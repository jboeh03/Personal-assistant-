## 1. The name — reversed 2026-08-05

### 🔁 This section previously argued the opposite. Read the reversal first.

**What this file used to say.** An 88-line section argued for **Westside Property Care** as the
customer-facing name and against WPC513, demoting 513 to a short mark used on paperwork,
tailgates, and file names. The reasoning: the name travels by ear over a fence, "513" has to
be spelled out and then explained, and a number in the name reads in a different commercial
register than the one that gets a card put on file.

**What the owner decided.** The other way. He shipped a finished logo and a signature block
carrying **WESTSIDE PROPERTY CARE 513** as the full customer-facing name
(`_source/2026-08-05-owner-decisions.md` §1, §2 §13).

**This is a reversal, not a refinement, and it is recorded as one.** The original argument was
not wrong about the mechanics — the name genuinely does have to survive being said out loud,
and 513 genuinely does have to be spelled. That cost is real and §1.4 below is how it gets
paid. What changed is that the decision is no longer open: the mark exists, it is printed, and
a brand document that argues with a finished logo is a brand document nobody will use.

---

### 1.1 Customer-facing name: **Westside Property Care 513**

This is the name on the truck door, the yard sign, the door hanger, the service agreement, the
Google Business Profile, the invoice, the email signature, and the first line of every piece of
copy.

**Tagline: Seasonal Outdoor Living & Property Care.** His words, from the logo sheet. It is
reproduced verbatim or not at all — see §1.5.

**The three things the 513 now does that the old argument undervalued:**

1. **It is already printed.** The logo is finished, the signature block is set, and the
   agreement he asks people to sign is headed *WESTSIDE PROPERTY CARE 513*. A public name that
   disagrees with the contract is a defect, not a preference.
2. **It disambiguates a generic construction.** "Westside" is common — there are Westside
   dentists, Westside auto shops, Westside plumbers. The original section conceded this and
   argued it did not matter for a six-client business. It matters more than that on one
   surface: the Google Business Profile, where a neighbour who half-remembers a name is
   searching. "Westside Property Care 513" is unambiguous in a way "Westside Property Care"
   is not.
3. **It reads as a place, not a number.** 513 is the area code for Cincinnati and its western
   suburbs. To the target buyer — a Green Township or Oak Hills homeowner (`CANON.md` §5) — it
   is not a decorative number, it is where they live. That is the part the original argument
   got wrong: it treated 513 as a style signal when locally it is a geography signal.

### 1.2 Full form, short form, and the one form that never appears

| Form | Where | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Westside Property Care 513** | First reference on any customer-facing surface, always | Never abbreviated on first reference. Never "WPC513" as the first thing a reader sees. |
| **WPC513** | Every reference after the first, and as the mark | One word, no space, no punctuation. `WPC513 · SERVICE REPORT · …` |
| **WPC 513** | **Inside the logo lockup only** | The mark sets it with an optical space. In running text it is always `WPC513`. See `visual-direction.md` §7. |
| **WPC** | **Never.** Not in a headline, not in body, not in a subject line, not in alt text, not in a URL slug, not in structured data. | It is not shorthand for anything. It is internal noise. |

**Why "WPC" alone is a hard ban and not a preference:** the mark's whole job is to be a single
locked unit. A "WPC" that appears without its 513 teaches the reader that the number is
optional, which is exactly the demotion the owner reversed. It also collides with nothing
useful — "WPC" is meaningless to a homeowner.

### 1.3 The other name rules — unchanged by the reversal

- **The company is never the subject of a verb in customer copy.** He is. See `voice.md` §2,
  rule 1 — first person singular, always. "I skimmed the pool and emptied both baskets," never
  "Westside Property Care 513 performed pool maintenance," and never "we."
- **The one exception is the service agreement**, where "Westside Property Care 513" (and
  "WPC513") is the contracting party. `_source/2026-08-05-owner-decisions.md` §2 is already
  written that way and must stay that way.
- **First reference resets per surface, not per session.** A door hanger, a yard sign, an email,
  and a web page each get the full name once. A reader who arrives on the pricing page did not
  read the homepage.

### 1.4 Paying the cost the original argument identified

The cost is real: **a name with a number in it does not survive being said once over a fence.**
Three mechanisms pay for it, and every downstream agent is bound by them.

1. **Never make a listener assemble it.** Spoken and in voicemail, it is *"Westside Property
   Care — five one three."* Digits, not "five hundred thirteen," and never "W-P-C-five-one-
   three" to a stranger.
2. **Where a name has one job, use the words.** The yard sign, the door hanger, and the
   vehicle lettering exist so a neighbour recognises the name later. On those three surfaces
   the **words carry the recognition and the 513 carries the identification** — full lockup,
   words legible at speed, digits legible on approach (`visual-direction.md` §7).
3. **Search and social carry both.** The GBP listing name, the `<title>`, and the Open Graph
   title all carry the full name. Someone who half-heard "Westside something, five-thirteen"
   must be able to find it typing either half.

### 1.5 The tagline — also a reversal

This file previously read: *"Never a tagline bolted to the name."* **He has one, and it is
good.** The rule inverts, with a boundary:

- The tagline is **Seasonal Outdoor Living & Property Care.** Verbatim. Do not rewrite it, do
  not shorten it, do not swap "&" for "and," do not add a verb.
- It is **not part of the logo lockup.** `_source/2026-08-05-owner-decisions.md` §1 lists the
  lockup elements and the tagline separately, and the lockup already carries a line of small
  caps. The tagline sits as its own line where it earns the space — masthead, letterhead,
  yard sign, GBP description opener — and is absent everywhere else.
- **It says what the business is, which is why it works.** "Seasonal" is a disclosure, not a
  softener: it tells the reader up front that this runs March through October. Nothing in the
  old objection to taglines ("Outdoor Living Perfected") applies to a line that is a factual
  description.
- The **fact line** under the name on a sign or a hanger is still a fact, not a slogan:
  *"Sixteen visits. Six properties. One person."*

### 1.6 🔶 Human action required before anything else prints

1. **Ohio name availability.** File to match the public name: **Westside Property Care 513
   LLC**. Availability is unverified here and must be checked against the Ohio Secretary of
   State business search before filing. If unavailable, the fallback is **Westside Property
   Care of Cincinnati LLC**, trading publicly as *Westside Property Care 513* — the public name
   does not change either way.
2. **Anything already printed at the old name or the old price.** `CANON.md` §10 item 1 flags
   that a flyer draft carried **$249**. If anything has gone out reading "Westside Property
   Care" without the 513, or carrying $249, he needs to know which and how many before the next
   print run.
3. **The domain, the GBP listing name, and the email signature** should all match the approved
   legal name before the first door hanger is printed, not after.

## 2. Positioning statement

> For a west-side Cincinnati homeowner who has put real money into a pool, a patio, or an
> outdoor kitchen and already pays other people to look after things, **Westside Property Care
> 513** is a six-property membership that puts the same person on the property **twice a month
> from March through October** — sixteen visits, up to two hours each — to keep the outdoor
> living area maintained and ready to use, and sends a short written summary the same day
> covering what was done and what was found.
>
> Unlike a lawn service, a pool company, or a handyman, he is not there to finish a task you
> called about. He is there on a schedule, on the whole outdoor living area, keeping a record.
> By the sixteenth visit he knows the property better than any of them ever will.

**The differentiator, and why it is credible:** the book is capped at six properties
(`CANON.md` §3). Six is few enough that he can hold all six in his head — which gate sticks,
which window wells fill, which pot on the north side dries out first, which downspout backs up
when the maple drops. A company with two hundred accounts cannot do that and does not try.
**The cap is not a marketing device applied to the positioning. The positioning is a
description of what the cap makes possible.**

The full argument — *the sixteenth visit* — lives in `messaging.md` §1. It is the replacement
for the retired "same price in January as in July," and it is the single most-cited line in the
brand.

## 3. The promise

> **Twice a month from March through October, I walk your outdoor living area, take care of
> what it actually needs that day within the two hours, and send you a short written summary
> the same day — including anything I found that isn't mine to fix.**

### Why every clause of it is keepable

| Clause | What makes it true | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Twice a month from March through October" | Service Season March 1 – October 31; two scheduled visits each month | Agreement §1, §8 |
| "Your outdoor living area" | The serviced area is identified and agreed at the walkthrough and recorded in the property file | Agreement §2; ⚠ `service-catalog.md` §4.3 (clause language still valid) |
| "What it actually needs that day" | *"WPC513 will use reasonable judgment to address the highest-priority maintenance items first"* | Agreement §5 |
| "Within the two hours" | *"up to two hours of on-site service per visit"* — and it is stated as a boundary, never as the thing purchased | Agreement §1; `CANON.md` §3 |
| "Short written summary the same day" | Checklist block 8: *"record completed services · note repairs / additional-service recommendations · send short service summary to customer"* | Checklist §8 |
| "Including anything that isn't mine to fix" | *"notification of observed maintenance concerns"* · *"notification of visible equipment warnings"* | Agreement §2, §3 |

**One thing got stronger on 2026-08-05.** The same-day written report was previously membership
standard 5, marked 🔶 RECONSTRUCTED and unconfirmed (`operating-model.md` §7, `decisions.md`
R-3). His own field checklist now ends every visit with *"send short service summary to
customer."* That is independent confirmation from his own document. The promise is safe to
make. It is still worth asking him to confirm the wording, but it is no longer a guess.

### What the promise deliberately does not say

Each omission is load-bearing. Do not add any of them back.

- **Not** "every task, every visit." The agreement says the opposite in terms: *"Completion of
  every possible task during every visit is not guaranteed."* That clause travels with the
  two-hour cap everywhere it appears.
- **Not** an arrival time. *"Arrival times may vary because of weather, prior appointments,
  traffic, emergencies, or property conditions"* (Agreement §8). Clients are told their route
  day, not their hour.
- **Not** a response window. No guaranteed response time and none may be implied.
- **Not** anything about the pool water. *"WPC513 does not guarantee water chemistry, water
  clarity, equipment performance, or pool-system operation unless separately contracted"*
  (Agreement §3). This is the most important sentence in the business and it belongs beside
  every description of pool work.
- **Not** year-round. Eight months. Say it.
- **Not** "nothing will go wrong." The promise is that he will find it and tell you, not that
  it will not happen.

## 4. Proof points

Grouped by whether he can make the claim **today**. Nothing below is invented. Nothing below
requires a customer, a review, or a year in business.

### 4.1 Structural proof — true from day one, checkable by the customer before paying

1. **Six clients, maximum.** The site states how many of the six are open and it is the real
   number. When it is zero it says MEMBERSHIP FULL and offers a waitlist (`CANON.md` §3).
2. **Sixteen visits a season, and he will tell you all sixteen dates.** March 1 – October 31,
   two a month, on a Monday or a Friday set at enrollment.
3. **The same person, every visit.** No crew, no dispatch, no subcontractor, no rotating tech.
   A fact about staffing, not a claim about quality.
4. **One price.** $279 a month, and there is no second number. A property with a pool and an
   outdoor kitchen pays exactly what a plain patio pays. There is no tier, no module, no
   add-on, and no configuration table to shop between.
5. **He tells you what he does not do before you sign — in his own document, in his own
   words.** The agreement's own pool disclaimer, the *"deep-clean nothing unless specifically
   scheduled"* line on his field checklist, and the exclusions in §6 below all exist in writing
   before the price is asked for. Almost nobody in this category does this.
6. **A written summary the same day, every visit,** with photos where they matter (Checklist
   §1, §8).
7. **Thirty days' written notice, either party, and you are done** (Agreement §12). No term
   commitment.
8. **You are told your route day, and told why you are not told an hour.** The reason is that
   he will not rush your property because someone else is waiting, and saying that out loud
   converts a limitation into the proof of a principle.

> **Retired 2026-08-05.** Proof point #4 used to read *"The price is published, and it is the
> same in January as in July."* That is now false — there is no January billing. It has been
> replaced above by the one-price fact, which is the surviving half of what it was doing. The
> argumentative work it did is now carried by `messaging.md` §1.

### 4.2 Earned proof — specific to him, true today

9. **Military roots.** From his own Trust/About note: *local, military roots, service, honesty,
   integrity, dependability* (`_source/2026-08-05-owner-decisions.md` §4). This is real, it is
   his, and the brand had nothing this strong before it.

   **How it may be stated:** plainly, once, as part of who he is. *"Local, with military roots."*
   Or in his own first person: *"I'm from the west side and I served."* It belongs on the
   Trust/About page and in the walkthrough conversation.

   **How it may never be stated — this is a hard rule.** Do not quantify it into a **rank**, a
   **branch**, a **number of years**, a **unit**, a **deployment**, a **theatre**, an **era**, a
   **discharge status**, or a **veteran-owned certification**. None of those have been
   confirmed. Do not use a service ribbon, a chevron, a star field, an eagle, dog tags, or any
   insignia as a graphic device. Do not write "veteran-owned business" as a badge, do not claim
   any veteran-owned discount or set-aside status, and do not put it in structured data.
   **"Military roots" is exactly the claim he made. Publish exactly that claim.**

   If he later supplies a branch or a length of service he can stand behind, this entry gets
   updated — by him, in `_source/`, not by an agent inferring it.

10. **He comes out of Tri-State Grill Cleaning, the family's grill-cleaning business**
    (`CANON.md` §1). He has cleaned grills and outdoor kitchens on west-side properties. That
    is real, relevant, and it is the honest version of "experience."
    **Constraint:** do not attach a number of years, a number of grills, or a customer count to
    this unless he supplies a figure he can stand behind. State the lineage; do not quantify it.

11. **He is starting the season already underway and pricing it honestly.** A client signing now
    gets a **pro-rated remainder of 2026 plus a locked founding rate for a full 2027**
    (`CANON.md` §3). He is putting his own price at risk for a year to compensate for having no
    track record. Present it as exactly that — a concession with a real cost — never as a
    "limited-time offer."

    > 🔶 **Flag, don't guess.** `CANON.md` §3 says "clients signing now." ⚠ The stale
    > `pricing.md` §2 says "the first 4 clients, locked 12 months." Whether the founding lock is
    > capped at four members is **unresolved**. Until `wpc-strategist` resolves it, copy says
    > *"clients signing now"* and never asserts a cap on how many get it.

### 4.3 Conditional proof — publishable only once true

**Do not publish any of these before the underlying fact exists.** Each has approved wording and
a banned alternative.

| Claim | Publishable when | Approved wording | Never say |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insurance | The general liability policy is bound and in force | "Insured — general liability." | "Licensed and insured," "fully insured," "bonded," "certified" |
| Legal entity | Ohio Articles of Organization are filed | "Westside Property Care 513 LLC, an Ohio limited liability company." | "Established," "since," anything implying age |
| Sample report | One real service summary exists | "Here's an actual summary from an April visit, with the address removed." | A "sample report" built from imagination |
| Photography | Real photographs of his own work exist | Caption naming the property type, the month, and what is happening | Stock photography, ever, as a final answer (`CANON.md` §10.6) |
| Reviews | Real reviews exist | The exact count and the exact platform: "Three reviews on Google." | "5-star rated," "highly rated," "trusted by homeowners," any count that is not the count |
| Military service detail | **He supplies it himself, in writing, in `_source/`** | Whatever he wrote, verbatim | Any branch, rank, span, unit, or veteran-owned status inferred from "military roots" |

**On "licensed":** Ohio does not license this trade. "Licensed" is not merely premature, it is
**wrong**. It never goes on anything. The one place the word may appear is quoting the
agreement's own carve-out — *"Regulated pesticide or chemical-application services are excluded
unless WPC513 holds any licensing legally required"* (Agreement §7) — which is a disclaimer, not
a credential.

### 4.4 Not available. Do not claim, imply, or design around.

Years in business · a crew, team, staff, or "our technicians" · certifications · awards ·
testimonials · review counts · before/after galleries of work that is not his · "trusted by X
families" · guaranteed response times · a satisfaction guarantee · **any guarantee of water
chemistry, water clarity, equipment performance, or pool-system operation** · multiple service
areas · any implication of growth, expansion, or franchising (`CANON.md` §1).

If a layout needs a testimonial slot to look finished, the layout is wrong. See
`visual-direction.md` §8.

## 5. The trust gap, and what actually closes it

The honest problem: he is asking a stranger for a card on file and gate access, and he has no
portfolio. Every instinct in marketing says to paper over that. Do not.

**The gap is closed by four things, in this order:**

1. **The walkthrough.** He is standing in the backyard naming things — the gate that sticks, the
   window wells, the hose bib on the north side. Forty minutes of specificity beats any
   testimonial, and it is available on day one.
2. **The exclusions, said before the price.** A vendor who tells you what he will not do,
   unprompted, before quoting, is making a costly signal. His own two-page checklist is footed
   *"Maintain everything. Deep-clean nothing unless specifically scheduled"* — he wrote the
   scope limit on the document he carries, not just the one you sign.
3. **The absence of a lock-in.** Thirty days' written notice, either party. The risk of being
   wrong about him is one month of service.
4. **The founding rate.** The remainder of 2026 pro-rated, and the rate locked for all of 2027.
   He carries the risk of his own price for a year.

**The sentence that does the work, and it should be said plainly:**

> This is new. I'd be starting with you. That's why I'm pro-rating the rest of this season and
> locking your rate for all of next year, and why there's no term — thirty days' notice and
> you're done. I'd rather earn the second season than trap you in it.

## 7. What the brand is for, per surface

The site's job is narrow and `CANON.md` §6 already defined it. This table exists so no
downstream agent invents a fifth job for a surface.

| Surface | Its one job | Not its job |
|---|---|---|
