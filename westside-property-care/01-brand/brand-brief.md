## 1. The name — decided

### Customer-facing name: **Westside Property Care**

Not WPC513. This is the name on the truck door, the yard sign, the door hanger, the service
agreement, the Google Business Profile, the invoice, the email signature, and the first line
of every piece of copy.

**Why, in the order the reasons actually matter:**

1. **It survives being said out loud over a fence.** `CANON.md` §5 is explicit that the site
   is not a lead engine — it closes someone who *already heard his name*, from a neighbor, a
   yard sign, a grill-cleaning job, or a Google listing. Three of those four channels
   transmit the name by ear. "Westside Property Care" needs no explanation and no spelling.
   "WPC513" has to be spelled, and then explained, and then spelled again.

2. **It says what it is and where.** Two of the three words a qualified prospect would
   actually use are in the name. He needs six clients out of a few hundred qualifying homes
   in Green Township, Oak Hills, upper Delhi, and Covedale (`00-model/ideal-client.md` §3) —
   people who already think of themselves as living on the west side.

3. **The register is right for the buyer.** The buyer is a homeowner who already pays other
   people to maintain things and is choosing between vendors. "513" as a standalone company
   name reads as apparel, tattoo, or auto-detailing signaling — a different register than the
   one that gets a card put on file for $329 a month. That is an observation about
   convention, not about the area code.

4. **The Google Business Profile wants the real business name.** Local relevance comes from
   the address, the category, and the service area — not from a number in the name. A
   listing named "Westside Property Care" is also the listing a neighbor can find after
   hearing it once.

**The one real cost, stated:** "Westside" is a common construction, and there are Westside
dentists, Westside auto shops, and Westside plumbers. That would matter to a business trying
to own a category. It does not matter to a business that needs six clients and gets all of
them by name, by referral, or by proximity. He does not need to win a search. He needs to be
legible on a truck door and correct on an invoice.

### Where **WPC513** still lives

WPC513 is not retired. It is demoted from *name* to **short mark** — and it is genuinely
better in that role than the full name is.

| Surface | Use |
|---|---|
| Tailgate, equipment cases, tool asset tags | **WPC513** stencil alone |
| Hat, jacket, work shirt sleeve | **WPC513** alone |
| Truck door | Wordmark primary; **WPC513** as the secondary mark on the rule beneath it |
| Yard sign | Wordmark primary; **WPC513** on the lower rail |
| Service report header and footer | **WPC513** as the record stamp |
| Invoice and estimate numbering | `WPC513-2026-0114` |
| Property record and file naming | `WPC513 · PROPERTY 03 · REPORT 2026-04-06` |
| Internal folders, repo names, systems | **WPC513** |

The number earns its place on the paperwork. A property file and a service report headed
`WPC513 · PROPERTY 03` look like a system that has been running for a while. "Westside
Property Care — Report" looks like a Word document. That is the whole argument for keeping
the mark, and it is a good one.

### Name usage rules — enforceable

- **First reference in any customer-facing piece is always the full name.** "Westside
  Property Care."
- **"WPC" alone never appears in customer-facing copy.** Not in a headline, not in body, not
  in a subject line. It is internal shorthand only. WPC513 is the mark; WPC is nothing.
- **The company is never the subject of a verb in customer copy.** He is. See `voice.md` §2,
  rule 1 — first person singular, always. "I skimmed the pool," never "Westside Property Care
  skimmed the pool," and never "we."
- **The one exception is the service agreement**, where "Westside Property Care" is the
  contracting party. `00-model/service-catalog.md` §4 is already written in that voice and
  should stay that way.
- **Never a tagline bolted to the name.** No "Westside Property Care — Outdoor Living
  Perfected." The line under the name is a fact, not a slogan: "Six properties. Two visits a
  month."

### 🔶 Human action required before anything prints

`05-playbook/step-04-brand.md` flags this and it stands: **the owner approves the
customer-facing name before anything prints or ships.** Two items he has to resolve, not this
document:

1. **Ohio name availability.** The recommendation is to file as **Westside Property Care
   LLC**. Availability is unverified here and must be checked against the Ohio Secretary of
   State business search before filing. If the exact name is unavailable, the fallback is
   **Westside Property Care of Cincinnati LLC**, trading publicly as *Westside Property
   Care* — the public name does not change either way.
2. **The domain and the GBP listing name** should match the approved legal name before the
   first door hanger is printed, not after.

## 2. Positioning statement

> For a west-side Cincinnati homeowner who has put real money into a pool, a patio, or an
> outdoor kitchen and already pays other people to look after things, **Westside Property
> Care** is a capped, six-client membership that puts the same person on the property twice a
> month to keep the outdoor living area ready to use — and sends a written report the same
> day covering what was done and what was found.
>
> Unlike a lawn service, a pool company, or a handyman, he is not there to complete a task
> you called about. He is there on a schedule, on the whole outdoor living area, keeping a
> record. That is why he catches the split hose bib in December instead of the flooded
> basement in February.

**The differentiator, and the reason it is credible:** the book is capped at six properties
(`00-model/operating-model.md` §2). Six is few enough that he can hold all six properties in
his head — which gate sticks, which window wells fill, which pot on the north side dries out
first, which downspout backs up when the maple drops. A company with two hundred accounts
cannot do that, and does not try. **The cap is not a marketing device applied to the
positioning. The positioning is a description of what the cap makes possible.**

### Short forms

Use these verbatim. Do not improve them.

| Length | Copy |
|---|---|
| One line | Six properties. Two visits a month. The same person, and a written report every time. |
| Yard sign / hanger | Pool deck, patio, and outdoor kitchen care, twice a month. Six clients, one person. |
| GBP description opener | Twice-monthly care for the outdoor living areas of six west-side homes — pool deck, patio, and outdoor kitchen — with a written report after every visit. |
| Verbal, at a door | I take care of six properties on the west side. Pool deck, patio, outdoor kitchen. Twice a month, and you get it in writing. |

## 3. The promise

> **Twice a month I walk your outdoor living area, take care of what it actually needs that
> day, and send you a written report the same day — including anything I found that isn't
> mine to fix.**

### Why every clause of it is keepable

| Clause | What makes it true | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "Twice a month" | Two scheduled visits per calendar month on an assigned route day | `service-catalog.md` §4.11 |
| "Your outdoor living area" | The serviced area is identified and agreed at the walkthrough and recorded in the property file | `service-catalog.md` §4.3 |
| "What it actually needs that day" | Work is prioritized to the property, not run down a checklist | `service-catalog.md` §1 |
| "Written report the same day" | Membership standard 5 | `operating-model.md` §7 |
| "Including anything that isn't mine to fix" | Membership standard 2 — report it especially when it isn't his | `operating-model.md` §7 |

### What the promise deliberately does not say

Each omission is load-bearing. Do not add any of them back.

- **Not** "every task, every visit." That is a checklist he will fail the first week it
  rains (`service-catalog.md` §1).
- **Not** an arrival time. Clients are told their route day, not their hour
  (`service-catalog.md` §4.11).
- **Not** a response window. There is no guaranteed response time and none may be implied
  (`service-catalog.md` §4.7).
- **Not** anything about the pool water. He does not maintain water quality
  (`service-catalog.md` §4.1).
- **Not** "nothing will go wrong." The promise is that he will find it and tell you, not that
  it will not happen.

## 4. Proof points

Grouped by whether he can make the claim **today**. Nothing below is invented. Nothing below
requires a customer, a review, or a year in business.

### 4.1 Structural proof — true from day one, and checkable by the customer

These are the strongest assets he has, because a customer can verify every one of them before
paying anything.

1. **Six clients, maximum.** The site states how many of the six are open, and it is the real
   number. When it is zero it says MEMBERSHIP FULL and offers a waitlist
   (`operating-model.md` §5).
2. **The same person, every visit.** There is no crew, no dispatch, no subcontractor, and no
   rotating tech. This is a fact about staffing, not a claim about quality.
3. **Everything that is *not* included is published before you are asked to sign.** The
   exclusions in `service-catalog.md` §4 go on the website and are walked out loud on the
   first visit, *before* the price (`ideal-client.md` §6, step 4). Almost nobody in this
   category does this.
4. **The price is published, and it is the same in January as in July.** No seasonal rate, no
   pause, no winter discount (`pricing.md` §3).
5. **A written report the same day, every visit, with photos of anything worth documenting**
   (`operating-model.md` §7, rule 5).
6. **Month to month. Thirty days' notice, in writing, and you are done.** No term commitment,
   including for Founding Members (`pricing.md` §4).
7. **You are told your route day, and told why you are not told an hour.** The reason is
   membership standard 1 — he will not rush your property because someone else is waiting —
   and saying that out loud converts a limitation into the proof of a principle.
8. **He will tell you what he does not do, and who to call instead.** Pool chemistry goes to
   a pool company. Anything needing a licensed trade gets referred out
   (`service-catalog.md` §4.1, §4.9).

### 4.2 Earned proof — specific to him, true today

9. **He comes out of Tri-State Grill Cleaning, the family's grill-cleaning business**
   (`CANON.md` §1). He has cleaned grills and outdoor kitchens on west-side properties. That
   is real, relevant, and it is the honest version of "experience."
   **Constraint:** do not attach a number of years, a number of grills, or a customer count
   to this unless he supplies a figure he can stand behind. State the lineage; do not
   quantify it.
10. **The first four members lock their price for twelve months** (`pricing.md` §2). He is
    putting his own price at risk to compensate for having no track record. That is a real
    concession with a real cost, and it should be presented as exactly that — not as a
    "limited-time offer."

### 4.3 Conditional proof — publishable only once true

**Do not publish any of these before the underlying fact exists.** Each has approved wording
and a banned alternative.

| Claim | Publishable when | Approved wording | Never say |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insurance | The general liability policy is bound and in force (`operating-model.md` §8) | "Insured — general liability." | "Licensed and insured," "fully insured," "bonded," "certified" |
| Legal entity | Ohio Articles of Organization are filed | "Westside Property Care LLC, an Ohio limited liability company." | "Established," "since," anything implying age |
| Sample report | One real service report exists | "Here is an actual report from a February visit, with the address removed." | "Sample report" built from imagination |
| Photography | Real photographs of his own work exist | Caption naming the property type, the season, and what is happening | Stock photography, ever, as a final answer (`CANON.md` §9.3) |
| Reviews | Real reviews exist | The exact count and the exact platform: "Three reviews on Google." | "5-star rated," "highly rated," "trusted by homeowners," any count that is not the count |

**On "licensed":** Ohio does not license this trade. "Licensed" is not merely premature, it is
wrong. It never goes on anything.

### 4.4 Not available. Do not claim, imply, or design around.

Years in business · a crew, team, staff, or "our technicians" · certifications · awards ·
testimonials · review counts · before/after galleries of work that is not his · "trusted by X
families" · guaranteed response times · a satisfaction guarantee · multiple service areas ·
any implication of growth, expansion, or franchising (`CANON.md` §1).

If a layout needs a testimonial slot to look finished, the layout is wrong. See
`visual-direction.md` §8.

## 5. The trust gap, and what actually closes it

The honest problem: he is asking a stranger for a card on file and gate access, and he has no
portfolio. Every instinct in marketing says to paper over that. Do not.

**The gap is closed by four things, in this order:**

1. **The walkthrough.** He is standing in the backyard naming things — the gate that sticks,
   the window wells, the hose bib on the north side. Forty minutes of specificity beats any
   testimonial, and it is available on day one.
2. **The exclusions, said before the price.** A vendor who tells you what he will not do,
   unprompted, before quoting, is making a costly signal. `ideal-client.md` §6 orders the
   conversation this way on purpose.
3. **The absence of a lock-in.** Month to month, 30 days' notice. The risk of being wrong
   about him is one month of service.
4. **The Founding Member price lock.** Twelve months at the signing price, for the first
   four. He carries the risk of his own price for a year.

**The sentence that does the work, and it should be said plainly:**

> This is new. I'd be starting with you. That's why the first four lock their price for
> twelve months, and why there's no contract term — thirty days' notice and you're done. I'd
> rather earn the fifth month than trap you in it.

## 6. What this is not

The "what we are not" list, written in the voice it actually ships in — most of it should
appear on the site verbatim. The internal reason is in the right column.

| Not this | The internal reason |
|---|---|
| **Not a lawn service.** No mowing, trimming, edging, fertilizing, aerating, seeding, pruning, planting, mulching, or bed work. | "Property Care" sounds like lawn care to most people. Unsaid, the third visit turns into an argument. `service-catalog.md` §4.2 |
| **Not a pool service.** I skim the surface, empty the skimmer and pump baskets, and clear the deck. I do not test the water, add chemicals, vacuum, brush, backwash, or touch the filter — and I am not responsible for water condition. | The most important exclusion in the business. A homeowner seeing "pool" on an invoice twice a month will hold him responsible for green water in July. `service-catalog.md` §4.1 |
| **Not a landscaper.** No installation, no design, no hardscape construction. | Adjacent category, entirely different business. |
| **Not a handyman.** No repairs, no replacement parts, nothing requiring a licensed trade. | `service-catalog.md` §4.9 |
| **Not an emergency service.** No guaranteed response times, no promised arrival windows. Urgent requests get handled midweek when the schedule allows, and that is a real benefit — it is not a promise. | Without this, one bad storm week converts a membership into an unpaid on-call contract. `service-catalog.md` §4.7 |
| **Not a snow contractor.** I salt and clear ice on the patio, pool deck, and the walk serving them, on scheduled visits. Not the driveway, not the public sidewalk, not between visits. | `service-catalog.md` §4.8 |
| **Not a cleaning service.** Nothing inside — no interiors, garages, basements, enclosed porches, or sunrooms. | `service-catalog.md` §4.3 |
| **Not a home-watch or security service.** I am not checking your house while you travel. I am taking care of the outdoor living area on a schedule. | Adjacent category with different liability and different promises. |
| **Not a crew, and not growing into one.** One person. Six properties. That is the whole business and it is deliberate. | `CANON.md` §1 — never imply growth, scale, franchising, or crews. |
| **Not the cheapest.** A lawn service is less. A single grill cleaning is less. This costs what it costs and the price does not move. | `pricing.md` §8 — the membership price is never discounted. |
| **Not seasonal.** Same price all twelve months. The scope changes in November; the price does not. | `pricing.md` §3 — winter churn is the fastest way to kill this. |
| **Not for every house on the west side.** The route is built around two clusters, and a property that is not near one of them goes on the waitlist no matter what it pays. | `ideal-client.md` §3, §5.1. Say the geography plainly; never write copy aimed at Westwood, Price Hill, or Cheviot. |

## 7. What the brand is for, per surface

The site's job is narrow and `CANON.md` §5 already defined it. This table exists so no
downstream agent invents a fifth job for a surface.

| Surface | Its one job | Not its job |
|---|---|---|
| Website | Close someone who already heard his name. Make scope and price unambiguous; make joining or waitlisting frictionless. | Lead generation. SEO does not drive the architecture. |
| Door hanger / yard sign | Make a qualified neighbor recognize the name later and know it is nearby. | Sell. Nobody buys $329/month off a door hanger. |
| Google Business Profile | Catch the person searching his name or the category after hearing it. | Rank for the region. |
| Service report | Find, name, and price the next project. It is a sales document that reads like a record. | Be a receipt. `operating-model.md` §6.2 |
| Estimate | Give a flat number, itemize materials, and expire. | Negotiate. |
| Membership-full notice | Tell the truth about the schedule and route the prospect to the waitlist and to project work. | Manufacture urgency. |
