## 1. The one thing

> **The same person is on your property twice a month, and you get it in writing.**

That is the whole message. Every other line in this document supports it, and any line that
does not is cut.

**Why this and not something else.** The category's default message is a result — a clean
patio, a beautiful backyard. He cannot out-promise a landscaping company on results and should
not try. What he can do that no company with two hundred accounts can is *be the same person,
on a schedule, with a record.* `CANON.md` §2 says the membership's job is to put a trusted
operator on the property 24 times a year. The message is the model, said out loud.

**The "and you get it in writing" half is not a feature, it is the proof.** It is the only part
of the offer a homeowner can verify twice a month for the whole time they are a member, and it
is the mechanism that finds project work (`operating-model.md` §6.2). Never drop it to make a
line shorter.

**Short forms** — use verbatim, do not improve:

| Length | Line |
|---|---|
| Headline | Six properties. Twice a month. Written down every time. |
| Sub | The same person walks your outdoor living area twice a month, does what it needs that day, and sends you a report the same evening. |
| Spoken | I take care of six properties on the west side. Twice a month, and you get it in writing. |

## 2. The three supports

Each support answers a different question, carries its own proof, and has a stated failure
condition — the thing that would make it a lie. Downstream agents: if you cannot point at the
proof, do not make the claim.

---

### Support 1 — He knows *your* property

**The claim:** He can tell you which gate sticks, which window wells fill, and which pot on the
north side dries out first. Six properties is few enough to hold in his head.

**Why it matters:** `CANON.md` §7 — specificity is the entire brand, and it is the reason this
beats a big company. A route tech with forty stops cannot name a fixture on your property. He
can.

**The proof:**

| Evidence | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Six clients maximum, twelve visits a month | `operating-model.md` §2 |
| The same person every visit — no crew, no dispatch, no subcontractor | `CANON.md` §1 |
| A property file per home: access, pets, water and electrical, known issues, service history | `operating-model.md` §8, `_source/` §8 |
| A report that names things by name, every visit | `operating-model.md` §7, rule 5 |
| The serviced area is walked and agreed at the first visit and recorded | `service-catalog.md` §4.3 |

**The line:**
> Twenty-four visits a year on six properties. I know which gate sticks on yours.

**What makes this false:** a report that says "routine care completed." The claim lives or dies
in the report, not on the website.

---

### Support 2 — The scope is written down, including what is *not* in it

**The claim:** Everything he does not do is published before you are asked to sign anything —
and he walks it out loud before he gives you a price.

**Why it matters:** this is the highest-trust move available to a business with no track
record, it costs nothing, and almost nobody in the category does it. It also filters out the
client who would be a problem in month three (`ideal-client.md` §5.2).

**The proof:**

| Evidence | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Twelve written exclusions, precise enough to sign | `service-catalog.md` §4 |
| The exclusions are walked out loud *before* the price at the first visit | `ideal-client.md` §6, step 4 |
| The price is published, not quoted on request | `pricing.md` §1 |
| Same price all twelve months, no seasonal rate, no pause | `pricing.md` §3 |
| Materials always itemized at cost plus 25%, never folded into labor | `pricing.md` §6 |
| A signed agreement before the first visit | `operating-model.md` §8 |

**The line:**
> Everything I don't do is on this page, above the price. Pool chemistry, lawn, gutters, snow —
> I'll tell you who to call.

**What makes this false:** an exclusion that appears only in the agreement and not on the
website, or a price page that says "contact us."

---

### Support 3 — Six, and it is a real cap

**The claim:** The book stops at six properties. When they are full the site says MEMBERSHIP
FULL and offers a waitlist that is an actual list.

**Why it matters:** `operating-model.md` §5 — the open capacity is not slack he failed to sell,
it is part of the product. It buys the weather float, the extra hour on a property that needs
it, the estimate inside 48 hours, and the ability to keep membership standard rule 1: never
rush a property because someone else is waiting.

**The proof:**

| Evidence | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Six properties is twelve visits a month across four route days | `operating-model.md` §2 |
| Routine visits only Monday and Friday of weeks 1 and 3 | `CANON.md` §3 |
| Tuesday through Thursday held for projects, estimates, and urgent requests | `operating-model.md` §3 |
| A prospect off the route goes on the waitlist even at $329 | `ideal-client.md` §4 |
| The site shows the true count of open slots | `operating-model.md` §5 |

**The line:**
> Six is the cap because six is what fits in four route days without me hurrying at your house
> to get to somebody else's.

**What makes this false:** a seventh client. `operating-model.md` §5 — never quietly take one
because someone was persistent. The moment that happens, this support and the whole positioning
go with it.

## 3. Message hierarchy per surface

One job per surface (`brand-brief.md` §7). One ask per surface (`voice.md` §1, CTA rules).

| Surface | Lead with | Then | Then | The one ask |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Homepage, above the fold** | The one thing | Open slot count, or MEMBERSHIP FULL | Not lawn care, not pool service | Text me your cross street |
| **Homepage, scope section** | Support 2 | Green Season work, Dormant Season work | The exclusions, in full | — |
| **Pricing page** | What your property has, $289 and $329 first | Same price twelve months; card on file; 30 days' notice, no term | The exclusions again, above the fold on this page | Walk the property with me — about forty minutes |
| **Membership-full notice** | MEMBERSHIP FULL, plainly | The mechanism: twelve visits, four route days, route proximity | Project work Tuesday–Thursday, with the two published prices | Add your name to the waitlist |
| **Door hanger** | The one thing, four lines | Open slot count | — | {{phone}} |
| **Yard sign** | Name, then "Pool deck, patio, outdoor kitchen. Six clients, one person." | — | — | {{phone}} |
| **Google Business Profile** | Twice-monthly outdoor living care, six west-side homes, written report | Green Township, Oak Hills, Delhi, Covedale | Grill and outdoor kitchen deep cleans from $249 | Call or message |
| **Service report** | What was done, named and located | What was found, located and priced | Nothing else | Say the word and I'll add it |
| **Estimate** | The flat number | Materials separate and itemized | Good for 30 days | Reply yes |
| **First reply to an inquiry** | Two qualifying questions | Six clients, route built on clusters | What the walkthrough is and how long | Answer the two questions |

### The section order that must not change

On any surface carrying a price, the order is:

1. What happens on a visit
2. **What is not included**
3. The price
4. How billing and cancellation work
5. The ask

The exclusions go **above** the price. `ideal-client.md` §6 orders the live conversation that
way for a reason, and the page should match the conversation. A page that quotes $329 and then
discloses that pool chemistry is excluded has already lost the trust the disclosure was
supposed to buy.

## 4. How to talk about price

Per `voice.md` §3, never fix the number of price points — `decisions.md` D-3 and R-1 leave one
configuration open and the owner has not resolved it. Write configurations as rows, never as a
count.

**The frame:**
> What you pay depends on what your property has.

**The rows, in this order** (`CANON.md` §3 — lead with $289 and $329):

| A property with… | Price |
|---|---|
| A pool | **$289 a month** |
| A pool and an outdoor kitchen | **$329 a month** |
| Neither — property care on its own | **$229 a month** |

**Directly under the rows, always:**
> Two scheduled visits a month. The same price in January as in July. Card on file, charged on
> the 1st. Thirty days' notice to cancel, and there's no term.

**Founding Member, when fewer than four have joined:**
> The first four members lock their price for twelve months from the day they start. That isn't
> a promotion — it's what I'm giving the people who take a chance on a business with no track
> record. After four it's gone, and members five and six pay the standard rate.

**Published project prices** (`pricing.md` §5.1) — these two, and only these two, appear in
customer-facing material:

- Grill and outdoor kitchen deep clean — **$249+**
- Window well cleanout — **$49+**

Everything else is "quoted after I look at it." Never publish the internal anchors in
`pricing.md` §5.3.

**Member project note, worth saying out loud:**
> Members don't get a discount on project work. They get scheduling priority and no trip
> minimum — a $49 window well is worth doing when I'm already standing in the yard, and it
> isn't worth a dedicated trip when I'm not.

## 5. Objection handling

Columns: what they say · what is actually behind it · what to say · what never to say · where
it comes from.

Every "what to say" line is shippable copy.

---

### 5.1 "Why wouldn't I just hire a lawn guy?"

**Behind it:** They have not yet understood that this is a different category. They are pricing
it against what a lawn service charges per cut, which is the wrong comparison entirely.

**Say:**
> A lawn guy cuts your grass. I don't cut grass at all — no mowing, no trimming, no edging, no
> beds. What I take care of is the part of the yard you actually sit in: the pool deck, the
> patio, the outdoor kitchen, and the hardscape. Skimming, baskets, furniture, counters, the
> grease tray, blowing it all clean, and in the winter the hose bibs and the covers.
>
> And the lawn guy isn't looking at anything. I walk the property twice a month and write down
> what I find, which is how you hear about the window wells in October instead of in April.
>
> Keep your lawn guy. He and I aren't doing the same job.

**Never say:** anything that positions him as better than a lawn service, or any line implying
he does lawn or landscape work. `service-catalog.md` §4.2.

**Source:** `service-catalog.md` §2, §4.2 · `brand-brief.md` §6

---

### 5.2 "That's a lot per month."

**Behind it:** Two different people say this. One is doing arithmetic and cannot get there —
that person may be a base-tier client, a project client, or a waitlist name, and the
conversation stays friendly. The other wants the $329 service at $250 — that person is a
disqualifier (`ideal-client.md` §5.3).

**Say:**
> It is. It's the same in January as it is in July, and it doesn't move.
>
> Here's what it buys that isn't obvious. It's twenty-four visits a year, not two. It's the
> same person every time, so by the third month I know your property. And it's the reason I
> catch things — the split hose bib in December, the downspout dumping against the foundation,
> the window wells filling up. Half the value is the visits and half is the fact that somebody
> is looking.
>
> If it isn't the right fit, I'd still be glad to do the grill for you. That's $249 and up, and
> it doesn't commit you to anything.

**Never say:** any discount, any trial rate, any "let me see what I can do." The membership
price is never discounted (`pricing.md` §8), including for the last slot.

**Source:** `pricing.md` §8 · `ideal-client.md` §5.3

---

### 5.3 "What do I actually get for two visits?"

**Behind it:** Fair and genuinely the hardest question, because the honest answer is "it
depends on the day." Answer it with the day, not with a list.

**Say:**
> Two scheduled visits a month, on your route day. On each one I walk the property before I
> start and again before I leave, and in between I do what will make the biggest difference to
> the property that day.
>
> In summer that's usually skimming and both baskets, wiping the furniture and the outdoor
> kitchen counters, emptying the grease tray, blowing the patio and pool deck, collecting the
> leaves and sticks, watering the pots, and spot-treating the weeds in the hardscape joints.
>
> It isn't the same list every time and I don't want to pretend it is. A visit in early April
> with the cover just off and winter grit everywhere looks nothing like a visit in late August.
> Both are full visits.
>
> Then you get a report the same evening — what I did, what I found, and what it costs if you
> want it handled.

**Never say:** a checklist implying every line happens every visit (`service-catalog.md` §1) —
it creates an expectation he fails the first week it rains. Never a visit duration
(`operating-model.md` §6).

**Source:** `service-catalog.md` §1, §2

---

### 5.4 "What happens in winter?"

**Behind it:** They suspect they will pay $289 in January for nothing. This objection is the
single biggest threat to the business, because a member who cancels in February takes the whole
Green Season with them (`unit-economics.md` §6).

**Say:**
> Different work, same price.
>
> November through March I'm doing the things that keep a hard freeze from costing you money:
> hose bibs shut off and drained, hoses disconnected and put away, the outdoor kitchen water
> line drained — that's the one that splits. Covers on the furniture and the grill, and checked
> again after a big wind. Salt and ice on the patio, the pool deck, and the walk out to them.
> Storm debris cleared. Downspouts checked from the ground to see where they're discharging.
>
> And it's the best look at your property all year, because the leaves are down and I can see
> everything. The March report comes with what the property needs in April and what each thing
> costs.
>
> The price doesn't drop, because the value doesn't. It just looks like less.

**Never say:** a winter discount, a pause, a seasonal rate, or "reduced winter service." Never
propose one, even as an option to be declined (`pricing.md` §3).

**Source:** `service-catalog.md` §3 · `pricing.md` §3 · `decisions.md` D-7

---

### 5.5 "Why is there a waitlist? / Are you really full?"

**Behind it:** Reasonable suspicion. Manufactured scarcity is everywhere and they have been
burned by it. The answer is the mechanism.

**Say:**
> Six properties is twelve visits a month, and I do routine visits only on Monday and Friday of
> weeks 1 and 3. That's four route days. Past six I'd either be adding a fifth route day or
> hurrying at your house to get to somebody else's, and I'm not doing the second one.
>
> The waitlist is a written list with your address on it, because when a spot opens it goes to
> whoever is closest to the route I'm already driving. I'll tell you honestly where you sit.
>
> Two things that actually move you up: a spot opening near you, or a neighbor of yours with a
> pool joining the list. If two of you are on the same street, that's a cluster and it changes
> the math.
>
> Meanwhile I do project work Tuesday through Thursday whether you're a member or not.

**Never say:** "spots are filling fast," "exclusive," "invitation only," or any count that is
not the true count.

**Source:** `operating-model.md` §2, §5 · `ideal-client.md` §4

---

### 5.6 "What if I need something that isn't included?"

**Behind it:** Either a genuine question, or the first probe of a prospect who wants unlimited
scope (`ideal-client.md` §5.2). The same answer handles both, which is why it can be said
warmly.

**Say:**
> Then I quote it, and you decide.
>
> Two things have published prices: a grill and outdoor kitchen deep clean is $249 and up, and
> a window well cleanout is $49 and up when I'm already there on a visit. Everything else —
> pressure washing, gutters, bed cleanout, drainage, storm work, spring open and fall close — I
> look at it, then send you a flat number the same day or the next morning. Materials are
> separate and itemized, at cost plus 25%.
>
> What I won't do is quietly fold a two-hour job into a visit and have you think it was
> included. I'll name it and price it, and most of the time people say yes.
>
> Members don't get a discount on project work. They get scheduling priority and no trip
> minimum, which is worth more.

**Never say:** "just let me know and I'll take care of it," or anything absorbing a project into
the membership (`operating-model.md` §7, rule 4).

**Source:** `pricing.md` §5, §6, §7 · `service-catalog.md` §5

---

### 5.7 "You're brand new. Why should I trust you?"

**Behind it:** The real objection under most of the others. It cannot be answered with
credentials because there are none. It is answered by transferring risk off them.

**Say:**
> You shouldn't yet. I'd be starting with you.
>
> What I'm not new at is outdoor kitchens — I've been cleaning grills and outdoor kitchens on
> west-side properties through my family's business, and this came out of that.
>
> Here's what I can do about the rest. The first four members lock their price for twelve
> months, so nothing we agree today can move on you for a year. There's no contract term —
> thirty days' notice in writing and you're done, Founding Member or not. Everything I don't do
> is written down before you sign anything. And you get a report after every single visit, so
> if I'm not doing the work you'll know within two weeks, not two years.
>
> The first month is the whole risk. If it's not right, tell me.

**Never say:** years of experience, "established," a client count, a testimonial, a review
count, or any credential that does not exist (`brand-brief.md` §4.4).

**Source:** `brand-brief.md` §5 · `pricing.md` §2, §4

---

### 5.8 "Can I pause it over the winter?"

**Say:**
> No, and I'd rather explain why than just say no. The flat price all twelve months is what
> makes this a membership instead of a seasonal service, and the winter work — the hose bibs,
> the covers, the ice, the full inspection — is the part that prevents actual damage. If I
> discounted January, the honest next question is why you're paying anything in January, and
> then we're both pretending the winter visits don't matter.
>
> If you only want warm-weather work, I'd genuinely rather sell you projects. Spring open in
> April, a grill deep clean before you host, pressure washing in May. That's a real option and
> I'm not saying it to be difficult.

**Never say:** anything that leaves the door open. No "let me think about it," no "maybe for
you." `pricing.md` §3.

**Source:** `pricing.md` §3 · `unit-economics.md` §6

---

### 5.9 "Can you come more often? / Can you come on Wednesday?"

**Say:**
> Two visits a month is the membership, and they land on your route day — the first and third
> Monday, or the first and third Friday, depending on which cluster you're in. I don't add
> visits and I don't do a specific requested date, because the day I start doing that is the
> day I start rushing somebody.
>
> Tuesday through Thursday I'm doing projects and estimates, and that's deliberately kept open.
> So if something comes up between visits, there's a decent chance I can get to it midweek. I
> won't promise same-day and I'd rather tell you that now than have you find out in February.

**Never say:** a response window, a guaranteed turnaround, or "just call me any time."
`service-catalog.md` §4.7, §4.11.

**But do not strip the reply commitment when you strip the response window.** They are
different promises and `voice.md` §8 splits them at items 14a and 14b. Promising that *work*
gets done inside a stated period is banned — "within 24 hours," "same-day service." Promising
that *he answers* is required wherever someone is left waiting, and it is said in his own
hours with the failure case attached: *"I'll answer the same day or the next morning. If it's
after Thursday, it might be Monday."* Answering a text costs him nothing he doesn't have; a
route day does.

**Source:** `service-catalog.md` §4.11 · `ideal-client.md` §5.4

---

### 5.10 "What time will you be there?"

**Say:**
> I'll tell you the day, not the hour. That's on purpose — if I give you an eleven o'clock, then
> the property before yours gets shortchanged when it needs an extra half hour, and eventually
> yours does too. Your visits are the first and third {{route_day}}. You don't need to be home.

**Never say:** a window, "between," "morning," or "I'll text when I'm on my way" as a standing
promise. `service-catalog.md` §4.11.

**Source:** `service-catalog.md` §4.11 · `operating-model.md` §7, rule 1

---

### 5.11 "Will you do the front too? / What about the garage?"

**Say:**
> The membership covers the outdoor living area — patio, pool and pool deck, outdoor kitchen,
> the hardscape around them, and the walkway that serves them. We'll walk it together on the
> first visit and write down exactly what's in and what isn't, so there's nothing to argue about
> later.
>
> Front of the house, lawn, wooded part of the lot, garage, sunroom, anything indoors — not in
> it. If you want something out there handled, I'll quote it.

**Never say:** "sure, I can grab that while I'm here." `service-catalog.md` §4.3.

**Source:** `service-catalog.md` §4.3

---

### 5.12 "Do you handle the pool chemicals?"

**Say:**
> No. I skim the surface, empty the skimmer and pump baskets, and clear the deck and the
> surround. That's it, and it's twice a month.
>
> I don't test the water, add anything to it, vacuum, brush, backwash, or touch the filter, and
> I don't open or close the pool. If your water goes green in July, that's not something I'm
> responsible for or able to fix.
>
> You want a pool company for that — around here it runs about $121 to $142 a month for weekly
> service with chemicals. Different product from what I do, and worth having.

**Never say:** anything softening this. It is the most important exclusion in the business.

**Source:** `service-catalog.md` §4.1

---

### 5.13 "What if it rains on my day?"

**Say:**
> I move you, usually to the following Monday or Friday — weeks 2 and 4 are kept open for
> exactly that. Same visit, same month, nothing changes on the billing. I'll tell you the day
> I'm moving you to, not the day I decided to.

**Source:** `operating-model.md` §3 · `service-catalog.md` §4.11

---

### 5.14 "Who else do you work for?"

**Say:**
> I'm not going to name clients — I wouldn't name you either. What I'll tell you is how many:
> {{slots_open}} of the six spots are open right now. If a neighbor of yours refers you, ask
> them; that's their call to make, not mine.

**Never say:** a client name, a street, or an identifying detail without written permission.
Never a made-up "we serve homes in…" claim.

**Source:** `voice.md` §6.7 · `brand-brief.md` §4.4

---

### 5.15 "Can I get a discount for paying a year up front?"

**Say:**
> I appreciate it, but no. The price is the price, and it's the same for everybody in the same
> configuration. There's no annual rate and no discount for the last slot.
>
> What I do have is the Founding rate — the first four members lock their price for twelve
> months. If you're one of the first four, that's yours automatically.

**Never say:** any prepay discount, any bundled discount, any "for you." `pricing.md` §8.

**Source:** `pricing.md` §2, §8

## 7. Two things never to say, at any point, on any surface

1. **Anything implying growth.** No "as we grow," no "currently accepting," no "additional
   territories," no "expanding to serve more families." `CANON.md` §1 — this is a deliberate
   side income at 1.5–2 days a week, and it is not becoming anything else. Copy that hints
   otherwise makes the six-client cap read as a startup phase instead of the product.
2. **Anything aimed at Westwood, Price Hill, or Cheviot.** No copy, no ads, no yard-sign plan,
   no service-area line that includes them. `ideal-client.md` §3 — this is arithmetic, not a
   judgment, and an inquiry from one of those areas that qualifies on attributes and route is
   welcome. An *exception* is fine; a *strategy* is not.
