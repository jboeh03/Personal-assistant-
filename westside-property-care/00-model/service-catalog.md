# Service Catalog

**Owner:** `wpc-strategist` · **Status:** source of truth for what is included and excluded

**One price, one season, one scope. March 1 – October 31.** There is no second scope, because
there is no second season. Price in `pricing.md` §1.

> **Rewritten 2026-08-05.** The **Dormant Season section is deleted** — the membership does
> not run November through February and nothing in it is winter work. The pool scope is
> materially heavier than the superseded version: **vacuuming, brushing, backwashing, both
> baskets, the automatic-vacuum debris container, and salt/system indicator checks are now
> included.** Exclusion 4.1 was rebuilt around what is actually still excluded, which is
> **chemistry, chemicals, filter servicing, and any guarantee.** See `decisions.md` **D-20**
> and **D-22**.

## 1. The line that governs everything below

> ### **Maintain everything. Deep-clean nothing unless specifically scheduled.**

**These are his words, from the footer of his own field checklist, and they do more scope
protection than a page of exclusions.** Every list on this page is an application of that one
sentence. When a question is not answered by any clause below, answer it with this.

Two rules ride with it and neither is optional:

**1. Not every task happens every visit.** Work is prioritised to what the property needs that
day. A visit in early March where the patio is covered in winter grit looks nothing like a
visit in late August where everything is dialled in and the real work is skimming and
watering. Both are complete visits.

**2. Completion of every possible task during every visit is not guaranteed.** That is the
agreement's own language (§5). Highest-priority maintenance items come first, using his
judgment.

**How this is said to a customer:** *"Two visits a month, and each one goes to whatever the
property actually needs — not to running down a list."* **Never publish a checklist that
implies every line happens every time.** It creates an expectation he will fail on the first
week it rains.

## 2. The shape of a visit

**Two scheduled visits per month, March through October. Up to two hours of on-site service
per visit. Unused service time does not roll over.**

His field checklist budgets those two hours across eight blocks. This is the internal
structure of the visit — it is **not** a customer-facing promise and **not** a stopwatch on
his work (`operating-model.md` §7):

| # | Block | Window | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrival + property walkthrough | 0:00–0:10 | 10 min |
| 2 | **Pool care** | 0:10–0:40 | **30 min max** |
| 3 | Outdoor kitchen + grill area | 0:40–1:00 | 20 min |
| 4 | Patio + furniture | 1:00–1:20 | 20 min |
| 5 | Property cleanup | 1:20–1:35 | 15 min |
| 6 | Trash + plants + misc. | 1:35–1:45 | 10 min |
| 7 | Flexible priority block | 1:45–1:55 | 10 min |
| 8 | Final walk + documentation | 1:55–2:00 | 5 min |

Blocks 2 and 3 only exist if the property has a pool or an outdoor kitchen. A property with
neither runs about seventy minutes — and is not a target client for exactly that reason
(`ideal-client.md` §2).

**Block 7 is the most valuable ten minutes in the business.** It is where the homeowner's own
priority gets handled, where a heavier debris pocket gets cleared, and where the difference
between a service and a checklist actually shows up.

**Block 8 is where the money is found.** The `NOTES / ITEMS TO QUOTE / FOLLOW-UP` block at the
foot of the checklist is the project-finding mechanism the whole model depends on
(`CANON.md` §2).

## 3. What is included, March through October

As applicable to the property. Organised by the checklist blocks in §2.

### 3.1 Arrival and property walkthrough *(block 1)*

- Walk the outdoor living areas before starting
- Check for storm debris, leaves, sticks, trash, and obvious issues
- Visually check the pool area and equipment
- Check the outdoor kitchen and grill area
- Note anything unusual or anything requiring customer approval
- Quick **before** photos where useful

### 3.2 Pool care *(block 2 — up to approximately 30 minutes per visit)*

**This is materially more than the superseded scope. Read it against §4.1.**

- Skim the pool surface
- **Brush steps, corners, and selected areas**
- **Vacuum as needed, stopping at approximately 30 minutes of total pool time**
- Empty the **skimmer basket**
- Empty the **pump basket** when appropriate and safe
- **Empty the automatic pool-vacuum debris container** when applicable
- **Check displayed salt level and system indicators**
- **Check Inspect Cell / No Flow / other visible warnings**
- **Backwash if needed and appropriate for the system**
- Quick visual equipment inspection
- Notification of visible equipment warnings
- Clear debris from the pool deck and surround; straighten and wipe pool furniture

> ### **WPC513 does not guarantee water chemistry, water clarity, equipment performance, or pool-system operation unless separately contracted.**

**That sentence is the owner's, it is adopted verbatim, and it must appear wherever pool work
is described** — the agreement, the what's-included page, the leave-behind, and the
walkthrough conversation. It is repeated as exclusion §4.1 because it is the single clause
that protects this business.

### 3.3 Outdoor kitchen and grill area *(block 3)*

- Wipe countertops and accessible outdoor-kitchen surfaces
- Wipe stainless-steel surfaces
- Remove light food and grease buildup from accessible areas
- Empty the grease trap or drip pan when needed
- Wipe the grill exterior
- Check the cooking area for excessive buildup
- Look for obvious pest or nesting activity
- Note repairs or deep-cleaning needs

### 3.4 Patio and furniture *(block 4)*

- Wipe patio tables
- Wipe chairs and furniture as needed
- Straighten cushions and furniture
- Wipe frequently used surfaces
- Remove obvious cobwebs
- Light cleanup around seating areas

### 3.5 Property cleanup *(block 5)*

- Collect sticks and small branches
- Remove concentrated leaf and debris pockets
- Pick up visible trash
- Sweep or blow small designated areas where appropriate
- Check walkways around the outdoor living area

### 3.6 Trash, plants, and miscellaneous *(block 6)*

- Empty designated outdoor trash cans into the homeowner's containers
- Light wipe-down of cans when needed
- Water outdoor flower pots and container plantings when requested
- Handle small recurring homeowner priorities
- Check previously noted minor issues
- Light spot treatment of visible weeds in hardscape joints, walkway cracks, and along the
  edges of beds bordering the outdoor living area

### 3.7 Flexible priority block *(block 7)*

Whichever of these the property needs most that day:

- Extra pool attention
- A heavier debris pocket
- Outdoor kitchen or grill cleanup
- Furniture or patio detail
- Plants or a seasonal need
- A small approved maintenance item

### 3.8 Final walk and documentation *(block 8)*

- Final property walk
- Return gates and areas as found
- **After** photos
- Record completed services
- Note repairs and additional-service recommendations
- **Send a short service summary to the customer the same day** — `operating-model.md` §8,
  membership standard 5

## 4. Exclusions

**This is the most valuable section in this document.** It is what protects the price. Every
clause is written to be lifted more or less directly into the service agreement, so each one
draws a line a customer and an operator can both point at without arguing.

> **Legal note, stated once:** these are scope definitions, not legal drafting. The actual
> service agreement lives in `04-operations/` and should be read by a real attorney before
> anyone signs it. Nothing here creates or limits liability.

### 4.1 Pool — what is excluded now that so much is included

**This clause was rebuilt on 2026-08-05.** The superseded version was headed *"the most
important exclusion in the business"* and excluded **vacuuming, brushing, and backwashing** —
all three of which he now performs (§3.2). Continuing to exclude them would have made the
agreement contradict the checklist in the client's own hand.

**What remains excluded is narrower, and every bit as important:**

> **Pool care under this membership is the physical maintenance listed in §3.2 — skimming,
> brushing, vacuuming for up to approximately 30 minutes, emptying the skimmer and pump
> baskets and the automatic-vacuum debris container, backwashing when appropriate and
> compatible with the system, and visual checks of displayed salt levels, system indicators,
> and equipment warnings — performed on two scheduled visits per month, March through
> October. It is not pool service.**
>
> **Specifically not included:**
> - **Water chemistry.** Testing, balancing, or adjusting of any kind.
> - **Chemicals.** Chlorine, shock, algaecide, stabiliser, acid, salt, or any other product
>   added to the water. Chemicals are never supplied under the membership and are billed as
>   materials at cost + 25% if separately arranged (`pricing.md` §6).
> - **Filter servicing.** Cleaning, disassembly, cartridge replacement, DE charging, or any
>   filter work beyond backwashing a system designed to be backwashed.
> - **Equipment work.** Pump, heater, salt cell, automation, plumbing, or automatic-cleaner
>   repair, replacement, or adjustment beyond what a displayed indicator can be read from.
> - **Seasonal pool work.** Opening, closing, cover installation or removal, and winterisation.
> - **Below-surface and structural work.** Leak detection, liner or surface repair, tile and
>   waterline scale removal, acid washing, and any work on or in the pool below the water
>   surface other than the vacuuming and brushing described above.
> - **Anything during November 1 – end of February.** The membership does not run then.
>
> ### **WPC513 does not guarantee water chemistry, water clarity, equipment performance, or pool-system operation unless separately contracted.**

**Why this clause matters more now, not less.** Dedicated pool service in Cincinnati runs
**$121–142 a month for four visits including chemicals** — **43–51% of the $279 membership**,
at twice the visit frequency. Under the superseded scope that comparison was easy to wave off,
because the two products barely overlapped. **They overlap now.** The honest answer is the
disclaimer, said out loud on the walkthrough and not buried in a clause:

> *"I vacuum it, I brush it, I empty the baskets, I'll backwash it and I'll tell you if the
> cell throws a code. I don't do your chemistry and I don't guarantee your water. I maintain
> the pool as part of the whole outdoor space. If you want somebody responsible for the water,
> you want a pool company, and I'll tell you who I'd call."*

That answer costs a few sales and prevents every bad year. It is also, per `CANON.md` §5, the
kind of straight answer that makes people trust the rest of the document.

> **Insurance note — `CANON.md` §10.3.** Vacuuming, backwashing, and salt-cell checks are
> **pool-system operation**. The superseded exclusion said he did none of it. **His insurer
> has to be told what he actually touches before the first paid visit.** This is not a
> scope question; it is a coverage question, and it is open.

### 4.2 Lawn and landscape — say it before they ask

> **The membership does not include lawn or landscape service of any kind.** Not included:
> mowing, trimming, edging, blowing of lawn areas, fertilisation, aeration, overseeding,
> weed-and-feed, pruning, shrub or tree trimming, planting, mulching, bed edging, bed
> cleanout, or landscape installation of any kind.
>
> Weed treatment under the membership is limited to **spot treatment of visible weeds in
> hardscape joints, walkway cracks, and along the edges of beds bordering the outdoor living
> area.** Weeding inside planting beds is a quoted project.
>
> **Regulated pesticide or chemical-application services are excluded** unless WPC513 holds
> any licensing legally required (agreement §7).

**Why:** "Property Care" sounds like lawn care to most people. If this isn't in writing and
said out loud on the first walkthrough, the third visit turns into an argument.

### 4.3 The outdoor living area is the boundary

> **The membership covers the exterior outdoor living area** — patio, pool and pool deck,
> outdoor kitchen, adjoining hardscape, and the walkways serving them. **The specific area
> covered is identified and agreed at the initial walkthrough and recorded in the property
> file.**
>
> **Not included:** any interior space, including garages, basements, enclosed porches, and
> sunrooms; the front of the property except the walkway serving the outdoor living area; lawn
> areas; wooded or unmaintained portions of the lot; outbuildings; and any area not identified
> at the walkthrough.

### 4.4 Height and equipment

> **No work is performed from a ladder, from a roof, or above roof line.** Gutter and
> downspout inspection is done from the ground — visually, and by observing discharge.
>
> **Work requiring a pressure washer, a ladder, a chainsaw, powered digging equipment, a
> trailer, or any chemical beyond routine surface cleaner and spot weed treatment falls
> outside the membership** and is quoted separately.

**Why the equipment line matters:** it is the cleanest possible test. Both parties can look at
the truck and settle it in three seconds. If it needs something he didn't bring on a route
day, it is a project.

### 4.5 Deep cleaning — the grill exclusions, kept as the owner wrote them

Agreement §4, kept intact:

> **Grill and outdoor kitchen care under the membership is** wiping countertops and accessible
> surfaces, light cleaning around cooking equipment, removal of accessible grease or food
> residue, grease-trap or drip-pan emptying when appropriate, and general visual inspection.
>
> **Professional grill deep cleaning, repairs, replacement parts, restoration, and equipment
> disassembly are not included** and may be quoted separately. Specifically not included:
> cleaning of burners, grates, flavorizer bars, heat shields, interiors, hoods, or vents;
> degreasing; and any disassembly of cooking equipment. That is a **grill and outdoor kitchen
> deep clean, from $249** — see `pricing.md` §5.1.
>
> **Pressure washing of any surface is not included** and is quoted separately.

### 4.6 Volume limits on debris and waste — two limits, and both are needed

> **Included:** hand-collected loose leaves, sticks, and debris from the outdoor living area,
> transferred to the homeowner's designated waste containers, **up to the capacity of those
> containers**, and **within the two-hour scheduled service time.**
>
> **Not included:** debris exceeding the capacity of the homeowner's containers; debris
> requiring more time than the scheduled visit allows; removal of any waste from the property;
> moving containers to or from the curb; hauling; disposal; and dump fees.
>
> **Large-volume debris removal and hauling are quoted, with disposal billed at cost + 25%.**

**Reconsidered 2026-08-05, because the old reasoning no longer holds.** The container-capacity
limit was originally chosen *specifically to avoid a time limit*, on the argument that a time
limit would put a stopwatch back into the business. **There is now a time limit**, in the
agreement, in the owner's own words. So the old justification is dead and the clause needed a
new one.

**Both limits stay, because they fail in different directions:**

| | What it answers | When it binds first |
|---|---|---|
| **Container capacity** | *How much can leave the property?* | Post-storm, or a heavy October leaf drop, when the can fills in twenty minutes |
| **Two-hour cap** | *How long is he here?* | A pool day where blocks 2 and 3 take fifty minutes and there is no time left for a debris pocket |

Neither substitutes for the other. **Whichever binds first, binds** — and the flexible priority
block (§3.7) plus his judgment decide what gets done with the time that remains. Stating both
is more honest than stating either, and the customer can verify both without argument: they
can see their own can, and they know when he arrived.

### 4.7 Storm response, and what "emergency" means

> **Included:** clearing storm debris from the outdoor living area on the next scheduled visit,
> by hand, within the limits in §4.6.
>
> **Not included:** any storm response requiring a saw, a trailer, more than one trip, or more
> than the homeowner's container capacity; tree or large-limb removal; and structural or
> property damage repair.
>
> **Westside Property Care 513 is not an emergency service and does not offer guaranteed
> response times.** Urgent requests are accommodated when the schedule allows — Tuesday
> through Thursday is held open in part for exactly this — but no response window is promised
> and none should be inferred.

**Why:** this is the clause that keeps a $279 membership from turning into an unpaid on-call
contract the first week of a bad storm season.

### 4.8 The off-season — November 1 through end of February

> **The membership runs March 1 – October 31 only.** During November, December, January, and
> February there are **no scheduled visits, no property checks, no storm response, no freeze
> protection, no snow or ice service of any kind, and no membership charge.**
>
> Any work performed during those months is a **separately arranged and separately quoted
> project.**

**This clause replaces the entire Dormant Season section of the superseded catalog and it
replaces the snow-and-ice clause with it.** Say it on the walkthrough, in those words, before
the signature. A member who signs in August and discovers in November that nobody is coming
did not read a clause — he was not told.

**A separate, optional winter offering exists** (`03-marketing/winter-service.md`). **It is not
part of this membership.** It is never described as included, never bundled into the $279, and
never implied by a phrase like "year-round care."

### 4.9 Repairs, parts, and trades

> **Westside Property Care 513 does not perform repairs, replace parts, or install, alter, or
> remove any fixed element of the property under the membership.**
>
> Anything requiring a replacement part, a permit, or a licensed trade — electrical, plumbing,
> gas, HVAC, structural, or pool equipment — is quoted separately, and may be referred to a
> licensed contractor.
>
> **Replacement parts and materials are always billed separately at cost + 25%.**
>
> **WPC513 is not responsible for pre-existing damage, deterioration, hidden defects,
> improperly installed equipment, equipment failure, normal wear, or conditions outside its
> control** (agreement §10). Potential concerns observed during service are communicated when
> reasonably practical — see membership standard 2.

### 4.10 Watering and plant care

> **Included:** watering of flower pots and container plantings within the outdoor living
> area, on scheduled visits, when requested.
>
> **Not included:** lawn or garden bed irrigation; irrigation system operation, repair, or
> winterisation; and plant care between scheduled visits, including vacation plant watering.

### 4.11 Visit frequency, scheduling, and the two-hour cap

> **The membership is two scheduled visits per calendar month, March through October**,
> performed on the route day assigned to the property — a Monday or a Friday.
>
> **Each visit includes up to two hours of on-site service. Unused service time does not roll
> over.** Because each visit is limited, WPC513 uses reasonable judgment to address the
> **highest-priority maintenance items first**, and **completion of every possible task during
> every visit is not guaranteed.**
>
> **Not included:** additional visits; on-demand visits; visits on a specific requested date;
> guaranteed arrival times; weekend service; and any accumulation or carry-forward of unused
> time.
>
> Arrival times may vary because of weather, prior appointments, traffic, emergencies, or
> property conditions. **Weather-affected visits may be moved to the next reasonably available
> service date**, within the same calendar month where possible.

**Why arrival times are excluded:** promising a time window is the fastest way to break
membership standard 1 — *never rush a property because another client is waiting.* Clients are
told their route day, not their hour.

### 4.12 Access, pets, and third parties

> The homeowner provides reasonable and safe access to the serviced area on the assigned route
> day, including gates, outdoor kitchens, pool equipment, electrical outlets, and water
> sources. **Pets must be secured when necessary for safe service.**
>
> Westside Property Care 513 is not responsible for work that cannot be performed because
> access was unavailable, and does not assume responsibility for conditions created by other
> contractors, household members, or third parties on the property.
>
> A visit that cannot be performed for lack of access is not refunded or made up outside the
> scheduled route days.

### 4.13 Work beyond the scheduled service

> **Work beyond the scheduled service time or outside routine service requires client approval
> before any additional billable work is performed** (agreement §6).
>
> Examples: professional grill deep cleaning, pressure washing, extensive storm or leaf
> cleanup, equipment or pool repairs, replacement parts, landscaping projects, hauling,
> specialty cleaning, and significant seasonal opening or closing work.

## 5. The exclusions list, condensed

For the walkthrough conversation and the one-page leave-behind. Everything here is a **quoted
project** or **not offered** — never a membership inclusion:

| Not included in the membership | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| **Pool water chemistry, chemicals, salt, filter servicing, equipment repair, open/close** | Refer to a pool service · quoted if separately contracted |
| **Any guarantee of water chemistry, clarity, equipment performance, or pool-system operation** | **Never offered** |
| Mowing, trimming, edging, fertilisation, planting, mulching, pruning | Refer out or quote |
| Grill deep cleaning — burners, grates, interiors, disassembly | **$249+** deep clean |
| Pressure washing, any surface | Quoted |
| Gutter cleaning (ground-level inspection is included) | Quoted |
| Window well cleanouts | **$49+** during a visit |
| Major storm cleanup — saws, trailers, tree work, multiple trips | Quoted |
| Debris beyond container capacity or beyond the two-hour visit; hauling; disposal | Quoted + disposal at cost + 25% |
| Bed weeding, bed cleanout, mulch | Quoted |
| Drainage installation and correction | Quoted |
| Repairs, replacement parts, licensed trades | Quoted or referred out |
| **November–February: visits, checks, freeze protection, snow, ice** | **Not offered.** Separate winter offering only. |
| Interior spaces, garages, outbuildings | Not offered |
| Extra visits, on-demand visits, guaranteed arrival times, rollover of unused time | Not offered |

**The principle underneath all of it, from the draft and worth repeating verbatim:**

> **The recurring service should find additional work, not absorb additional work.**

And the owner's own line, which says the same thing in six words:

> **Maintain everything. Deep-clean nothing unless specifically scheduled.**
