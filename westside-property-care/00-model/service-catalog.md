# Service Catalog

**Owner:** `wpc-strategist` · **Status:** source of truth for what is included, excluded, and seasonal

One price, two scopes. **Green Season runs April through October. Dormant Season runs
November through March.** The price is the same in both — see `pricing.md` §3.

---

## 1. The rule that governs every list below

**Not every task happens every visit. Work is prioritized to what the property needs
that day.**

This is the draft's own rule and it is the most important sentence in this document. The
membership is not a checklist that gets completed. It is a competent person deciding what
will make the biggest difference to this property today, and doing that.

A visit in early April where the pool cover is off and the patio is covered in winter grit
looks nothing like a visit in late August where everything is already dialed in and the
real work is skimming and watering. Both are complete visits.

**How this is said to a customer:** "Two visits a month, and each one is spent on whatever
the property actually needs — not on running down a list." Never publish a checklist that
implies every line happens every time. It creates an expectation he will fail on the first
week it rains.

## 2. Green Season — April through October

**14 route days across seven months.** The property is in use, so the work is about keeping
it ready to enjoy.

### Included, as applicable to the property

**Inspection**
- Walk the property on arrival, before starting
- Look for storm damage, standing water, fallen limbs, drainage problems, pool issues,
  outdoor kitchen problems, furniture damage, anything visibly out of place
- Photograph anything that deserves documentation
- Second walkthrough before leaving

**Pool area** *(Pool Care module — see §4 for exactly what this is and is not)*
- Skim the surface
- Empty the skimmer basket and the pump basket
- Clear debris from the pool deck and surround
- Straighten and wipe pool furniture

**Patio and outdoor living area**
- Wipe down patio furniture and table surfaces
- Straighten and reset furniture and cushions
- Blow hard surfaces clean — patio, walkways, pool deck, outdoor kitchen area
- General organization of the outdoor living area

**Outdoor kitchen** *(Outdoor Kitchen Care module — see §4)*
- Wipe down outdoor countertops
- Wipe down stainless steel surfaces
- Empty the flat-top or grill grease trap and drip tray
- Clear debris from around and under the cooking area

**Grounds within the outdoor living area**
- Hand-collect loose leaves, sticks, and debris
- Empty outdoor waste into the homeowner's designated containers
- Water flower pots and container plantings
- Light spot treatment of visible weeds in hardscape joints, walkway cracks, and along
  bed edges

**Close out**
- Check that gates are latched
- Confirm all equipment and supplies are removed from the property
- After photos where useful
- **Service report sent the same day** — see `operating-model.md` §7, rule 5

## 3. Dormant Season — November through March

**10 route days across five months.** The property is not in use, so the work shifts from
presentation to protection. The single most valuable thing he does in January is notice
something in December that would have been a burst pipe in February.

### Included, as applicable to the property

**Water and freeze protection** — the highest-value work of the season
- Check hose bibs; confirm they are shut off and drained
- Disconnect and store hoses
- Drain and store or cover exposed lines and fixtures
- Check outdoor kitchen water lines, sinks, and shutoffs
- Check the pool equipment pad visually for anything obviously wrong
- Confirm that freeze-vulnerable items are covered, drained, or brought in

**Gutters and drainage** — **check, not clean; ground level only**
- Visually inspect gutters and downspouts from the ground
- Confirm downspouts are discharging and extensions are in place
- Look for overflow staining, ice damming, standing water, and pooling at foundations
- Report anything found. Actual gutter cleaning is a quoted project — §4

**Storm and debris**
- Clear storm debris from the outdoor living area
- Hand-collect fallen limbs and branches within routine scope
- Clear drains, grates, and window wells of surface debris

**Furniture and equipment**
- Cover, store, or consolidate outdoor furniture and cushions at the season transition
- Uncover and reset at the spring transition
- Cover and secure the grill and outdoor kitchen equipment
- Check that covers are still secure after high wind

**Ice and hardscape**
- Salt and clear ice from the outdoor living hardscape — patio, pool deck, and the
  walkway serving the outdoor living area — on scheduled visits
- Watch for salt damage to stone and concrete and adjust product accordingly

**Inspection and planning**
- Full exterior inspection with a photo report — the fullest inspection of the year,
  because the leaves are down and everything is visible
- Note and photograph anything for spring
- **Spring-prep planning:** the March report includes what the property will need in
  April, with prices. This is the single highest-converting report of the year and it
  should be treated that way.

### Why the price doesn't drop in winter

Because the value doesn't. Freeze protection, a monthly set of eyes on an unused property,
and a documented spring plan are worth more than skimming a pool — they just look like
less. The member is buying a year of coverage, not a season of visits. See `pricing.md` §3.

## 4. Exclusions

**This is the most valuable section in this document.** It is what protects the price. Every
clause below is written to be lifted more or less directly into the service agreement, so
each one draws a line a customer and an operator can both point at without arguing.

> **Legal note, stated once:** these are scope definitions, not legal drafting. The actual
> service agreement lives in `04-operations/` and should be read by a real attorney before
> anyone signs it. Nothing here creates or limits liability.

### 4.1 Pool — the most important exclusion in the business

> **Pool Care under this membership consists of skimming the pool surface, emptying the
> skimmer and pump baskets, and clearing debris from the pool deck and surround, on two
> scheduled visits per month. It is not pool service.**
>
> **Specifically not included:** water chemistry testing or balancing; adding chlorine,
> shock, algaecide, or any chemical to the water; vacuuming the pool; brushing walls or
> steps; cleaning, backwashing, or servicing the filter; pump, heater, or equipment repair;
> opening or closing the pool for the season; cover installation or removal; leak
> detection; and any work on or in the pool below the water surface.
>
> **Westside Property Care does not maintain pool water quality and is not responsible for
> water condition, clarity, or chemistry.**

**Why this clause has to be this blunt.** Full weekly pool service in Cincinnati runs
**$121–142 per month for four visits including chemicals.** The Pool Care module here is
**$60 a month for two visits with no chemicals.** It is manifestly a different product —
but a homeowner who sees "pool" on an invoice twice a month will hold him responsible for
green water in July unless the agreement says otherwise in words this plain. This clause is
also a sales asset: it tells the client exactly who to hire for water chemistry, which is
the kind of straight answer that makes people trust the rest of the document.

### 4.2 Lawn and landscape — say it before they ask

> **The membership does not include lawn or landscape service of any kind.** Not included:
> mowing, trimming, edging, blowing of lawn areas, fertilization, aeration, overseeding,
> weed-and-feed, pruning, shrub or tree trimming, planting, mulching, bed edging, bed
> cleanout, or landscape installation of any kind.
>
> Weed treatment under the membership is limited to **spot treatment of visible weeds in
> hardscape joints, walkway cracks, and along the edges of beds bordering the outdoor
> living area.** Weeding inside planting beds is a quoted project.

**Why:** "Property Care" sounds like lawn care to most people. If this isn't in writing and
said out loud on the first walkthrough, the third visit turns into an argument.

### 4.3 The outdoor living area is the boundary

> **The membership covers the exterior outdoor living area** — patio, pool and pool deck,
> outdoor kitchen, adjoining hardscape, and the walkways serving them. **The specific area
> covered is identified and agreed at the initial walkthrough and recorded in the property
> file.**
>
> **Not included:** any interior space, including garages, basements, enclosed porches, and
> sunrooms; the front of the property except the walkway serving the outdoor living area;
> lawn areas; wooded or unmaintained portions of the lot; outbuildings; and any area not
> identified at the walkthrough.

### 4.4 Height and equipment

> **No work is performed from a ladder, from a roof, or above roof line.** Gutter and
> downspout inspection is done from the ground — visually, and by observing discharge.
>
> **Work requiring a pressure washer, a ladder, a chainsaw, powered digging equipment, a
> trailer, or any chemical beyond routine surface cleaner and spot weed treatment falls
> outside the membership** and is quoted separately.

**Why the equipment line matters:** it is the cleanest possible test. Both parties can look
at the truck and settle it in three seconds. If it needs something he didn't bring on a
route day, it is a project.

### 4.5 Deep cleaning

> **Grill and outdoor kitchen care under the membership is the emptying of the grease trap
> and drip tray and the wipe-down of exterior surfaces and countertops.**
>
> **Not included:** cleaning of burners, grates, flavorizer bars, heat shields, interiors,
> hoods, or vents; degreasing; and any disassembly of cooking equipment. That is a **grill
> and outdoor kitchen deep clean, from $249** — see `pricing.md` §5.1.
>
> **Pressure washing of any surface is not included** and is quoted separately.

### 4.6 Volume limits on debris and waste

> **Included:** hand-collected loose leaves, sticks, and debris from the outdoor living
> area, transferred to the homeowner's designated waste containers, **up to the capacity of
> those containers.**
>
> **Not included:** debris exceeding the capacity of the homeowner's containers; removal of
> any waste from the property; moving containers to or from the curb; hauling; disposal;
> and dump fees.
>
> **Large-volume debris removal and hauling are quoted, with disposal billed at cost plus
> 25%.**

**Why a container-capacity limit rather than a time limit:** it is visible, it is the
homeowner's own can, and there is nothing to dispute. A time limit would put a stopwatch
back into the business, which `operating-model.md` §6 removes on purpose.

### 4.7 Storm response, and what "emergency" means

> **Included:** clearing storm debris from the outdoor living area on the next scheduled
> visit, by hand, within the volume limits in §4.6.
>
> **Not included:** any storm response requiring a saw, a trailer, more than one trip, or
> more than the homeowner's container capacity; tree or large-limb removal; and structural
> or property damage repair.
>
> **Westside Property Care is not an emergency service and does not offer guaranteed
> response times.** Urgent requests are accommodated when the schedule allows — Tuesday
> through Thursday is held open in part for exactly this — but no response window is
> promised and none should be inferred.

**Why:** this is the clause that keeps a $289 membership from turning into an unpaid on-call
contract the first week of a bad storm season. It also stays honest with the model: the
open Tue–Thu capacity really does mean urgent requests usually get handled, and that is a
genuine benefit — it just isn't a guarantee.

### 4.8 Snow and ice

> **Included:** salting and clearing ice from the outdoor living hardscape — patio, pool
> deck, and the walkway serving the outdoor living area — **on scheduled visits only.**
>
> **Not included:** driveway or public sidewalk clearing; plowing; shoveling of any area
> outside the outdoor living hardscape; and any snow or ice response between scheduled
> visits.

**Why:** without this, the first heavy snow converts a property-care membership into a snow
contract at no additional charge, on a day he had three other things scheduled.

### 4.9 Repairs, parts, and trades

> **Westside Property Care does not perform repairs, replace parts, or install, alter, or
> remove any fixed element of the property under the membership.**
>
> Anything requiring a replacement part, a permit, or a licensed trade — electrical,
> plumbing, gas, HVAC, structural, or pool equipment — is quoted separately, and may be
> referred to a licensed contractor.
>
> **Replacement parts and materials are always billed separately at cost plus 25%.**

### 4.10 Watering and plant care

> **Included:** watering of flower pots and container plantings within the outdoor living
> area, on scheduled visits.
>
> **Not included:** lawn or garden bed irrigation; irrigation system operation, repair, or
> winterization; and plant care between scheduled visits, including vacation plant watering.

### 4.11 Visit frequency and scheduling

> **The membership is two scheduled visits per calendar month**, performed on the route day
> assigned to the property — Monday or Friday of weeks 1 and 3.
>
> **Not included:** additional visits; on-demand visits; visits on a specific requested
> date; guaranteed arrival times; and weekend service.
>
> Visits may be rescheduled for weather or safety. A rescheduled visit is performed within
> the same calendar month where possible.

**Why arrival times are excluded:** promising a time window is the fastest way to break
membership standard rule 1 — *never rush a property because another client is waiting.*
Clients are told their route day, not their hour.

### 4.12 Access, pets, and third parties

> The homeowner is responsible for providing safe access to the serviced area on the
> assigned route day, including gate codes or keys, and for securing pets during the visit.
>
> Westside Property Care is not responsible for work that cannot be performed because
> access was unavailable, and does not assume responsibility for conditions created by
> other contractors, household members, or third parties on the property.
>
> A visit that cannot be performed for lack of access is not refunded or made up outside
> the scheduled route days.

## 5. The exclusions list, condensed

For the walkthrough conversation and the one-page leave-behind. Everything here is a
**quoted project**, not a membership inclusion:

| Not included in the membership | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Pool water chemistry, chemicals, vacuuming, filters, open/close | Refer to a pool service |
| Mowing, trimming, edging, fertilization, planting, mulching, pruning | Refer out or quote |
| Grill deep cleaning — burners, grates, interiors | **$249+** deep clean |
| Pressure washing, any surface | Quoted |
| Gutter cleaning (inspection from ground is included) | Quoted |
| Window well cleanouts | **$49+** during a visit |
| Major storm cleanup, saws, trailers, tree work | Quoted |
| Debris beyond the homeowner's container capacity; hauling; disposal | Quoted + disposal at cost + 25% |
| Bed weeding, bed cleanout, mulch | Quoted |
| Drainage installation and correction | Quoted |
| Repairs, replacement parts, licensed trades | Quoted or referred out |
| Driveway/sidewalk snow, plowing, between-visit snow response | Not offered |
| Interior spaces, garages, outbuildings | Not offered |
| Extra visits, on-demand visits, guaranteed arrival times | Not offered |

**The principle underneath all of it, from the draft and worth repeating verbatim:**

> **The recurring service should find additional work, not absorb additional work.**
