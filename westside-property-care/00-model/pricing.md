# Pricing

**Owner:** `wpc-strategist` · **Status:** the only source of truth for prices in this project

**Every price in Westside Property Care appears on this page. If a number appears anywhere
else in this repository and disagrees with this file, this file wins and the other document
is wrong.** Never invent a price. Quote this page verbatim.

## 1. The membership — three prices, built from three components

### Components (locked in `CANON.md` §3)

| Component | Price | Applies when |
|---|---|---|
| Property Care base | **$229/month** | Always — includes 2 scheduled visits per month |
| + Pool Care | **+$60/month** | The property has a pool |
| + Outdoor Kitchen Care | **+$40/month** | Grill, flat-top, or outdoor counters |

### The only three prices a customer ever sees

| Tier | Price | Property has |
|---|---|---|
| **Property Care** | **$229/month** | Neither a pool nor an outdoor kitchen |
| **Property + Pool Care** | **$289/month** | A pool |
| **Property + Pool + Outdoor Kitchen Care** | **$329/month** | Both |

$229 + $60 = **$289**.  $229 + $60 + $40 = **$329**.

### Lead with $289 and $329

$229 is the entry point. It is not the headline. The homes that can carry this service are
the ones with a pool or an outdoor kitchen in the ground — that is the whole targeting
thesis in `ideal-client.md`. Quoting $229 first trains the prospect to negotiate down from
a number that was already the floor.

Every customer-facing surface leads with the middle and top tiers and lets $229 sit
underneath as the base the others are built from.

### 🔶 The fourth configuration — outdoor kitchen, no pool

A property with an outdoor kitchen and **no** pool would compute to $229 + $40 = $269. That
would be a fourth price, and `CANON.md` locks the customer-facing list at three.

**Operating rule: Outdoor Kitchen Care is sold on top of Pool Care, not on its own.** A
qualified property with an outdoor kitchen and no pool is written at the base tier, **$229**,
and its outdoor kitchen surfaces are serviced within that visit. The $40 module is not
charged and is not discussed.

**CONFIRM WITH OWNER — this one has real money in it.** If the first members come off the
Tri-State Grill Cleaning customer list (which is *conditional* — see `CANON.md` §9.2), then
outdoor-kitchen-without-pool may be the *common* configuration, not the edge case. Three
such members would forgo $40 × 3 × 12 = **$1,440 a year**. The recommendation and the two
ways out of it are written up in `decisions.md` **D-3**. Do not resolve it by inventing a
price here.

## 2. Founding Member rate

**The first 4 clients lock their price for 12 months from their start date.**

- The lock covers the membership price only — the tier they signed at.
- It does not cover project work, add-ons, or materials.
- If a Founding Member adds a pool or an outdoor kitchen mid-term, the module is added at
  the standard rate and the new total is what carries forward.
- The lock is a real commitment and it is stated in the service agreement. It is also the
  strongest close available before there is a portfolio of finished work to point at: he
  is asking the first four people to take a chance on a business with no track record, and
  a 12-month price is a fair thing to give them for it.
- **After four members, the Founding rate is gone.** Members 5 and 6 pay the standard rate
  on standard terms. Do not extend it quietly to fill the last two slots.

## 3. Same price, all twelve months

**The price does not change in winter.**

The scope changes — Green Season April–October, Dormant Season November–March, both in
`service-catalog.md`. The price does not. This is what makes it a membership instead of a
seasonal service.

**Never propose a reduced winter rate.** A winter discount invites the member to ask why
they're paying anything in January, and winter churn is the single fastest way to kill this
business: a member who cancels in February takes the entire Green Season with them.
`operating-model.md` §6.3 measures retention on April 1 for exactly this reason.

## 4. Billing terms

| Term | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Payment method** | Card on file. Collected before the first visit. No exceptions. |
| **Charge date** | Automatically on the **1st of each month**, in advance. |
| **Billing period** | Calendar month. Two scheduled visits fall within it. |
| **Proration** | A member starting after the 15th is charged a half month for the partial month, then full months thereafter. |
| **Failed card** | Service continues through the current month. Card must be replaced before the next scheduled route day. |
| **Cancellation** | 30 days' written notice. Service runs through the paid month. Founding Members may cancel on the same terms — the lock is a price guarantee, not a term commitment. |
| **Pause** | No seasonal pauses. See §3. |
| **Projects** | Invoiced on completion, charged to the card on file unless the member arranges otherwise. |
| **Materials** | Itemized on the estimate and on the invoice, never folded into labor. See §6. |

A note on why card-on-file is non-negotiable: a six-client business cannot afford to spend
any part of its month chasing $289. The draft had no payment mechanics at all — see
`decisions.md` **D-5**.

## 6. Materials

**Materials are billed separately at cost + 25%, itemized.**

- Every material line appears on the estimate before the work starts and on the invoice
  after.
- Materials never disappear into labor revenue. If a project consumes $75 of materials, the
  client sees $75 + $18.75 = **$93.75**, and the books show it as its own line.
- The 25% covers sourcing, the supply trip, and carrying the cost until the invoice clears.
  It is not padding and it does not need to be apologized for — a Denver home-watch operator
  in the same category charges a 25% vendor markup on top of $400/month and $155/hour
  coordination.
- Disposal and dump fees are treated as materials: at cost + 25%.
- Routine consumables used on a membership visit — surface cleaner, trash bags, spot weed
  treatment, ice melt — are **not** materials. They are covered by the membership and
  budgeted as an operating expense in `unit-economics.md` §4.

## 7. How to quote a project

The point of this section is that he never has to guess with a customer standing next to him.

### The formula

```
  estimated on-site hours  ×  $100
+ materials at cost        ×  1.25
= subtotal, rounded UP to the nearest $25
```

**Floor: no standalone trip is quoted below $149.** A dedicated drive, setup, and teardown
costs the same whether the job is twenty minutes or ninety.

**The floor does not apply to work performed during a scheduled membership visit.** He is
already on the property and already paid to be there, so the trip is free. This is exactly
why the $49 window well works and why it would be a bad standalone job.

### Check the formula against the published prices

| Job | On-site hours | Calculation | Result | Published |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grill & outdoor kitchen deep clean | 2.5 | 2.5 × $100 = $250 | $250 | **$249+** ✓ |
| Window well cleanout, during a visit | 0.5 | 0.5 × $100 = $50 | $50 | **$49+** ✓ |
| Patio pressure wash | 2.5 | 2.5 × $100 = $250, round up | $250 | from $249 ✓ |
| Driveway + patio | 4.5 | 4.5 × $100 = $450 | $450 | from $449 ✓ |

The formula reproduces the published prices. That is the point — the price list and the
quoting rule are the same system, so a quoted job never lands below a published one.

### Why $100 an hour and not less

Cincinnati handyman labor runs **$40–80/hour**. He is not selling handyman hours. He is
selling a flat number for a finished result, with his own equipment, from someone the
client already trusts and already lets through the gate. That combination prices above
hourly labor, not at it. If a job pencils out below $80/hour of on-site time, the estimate
is wrong — either the hours are underestimated or the job shouldn't be taken.

### The five rules of quoting

1. **Never quote a flat number in the driveway from memory.** Walk it, measure it,
   photograph it. Send the number the same day or the next morning.
2. **If pressed for a number on the spot, give a range** — and set the low end where you'd
   still be glad to do the work. The final number lands inside the range you said.
3. **Quote a flat price, never an hourly rate.** An hourly rate invites the client to
   watch the clock and turns every efficiency he's earned into a discount he gives away.
4. **Materials are always a separate line.** Never bundle them to make a number look
   rounder.
5. **Underestimate the hours once and you've set the price for that job forever.** If the
   first pressure wash takes four hours and you quoted 2.5, the number to fix is next
   year's quote, not this year's invoice.

## 8. What is never discounted

- **The membership price.** Someone who negotiates the membership before the first visit
  will negotiate the scope after it. See the disqualifier in `ideal-client.md` §5.
- **The winter months.** See §3.
- **Project work for members.** See §5.3 — they get priority and no trip minimum instead.
- **The last slot.** Filling slot 6 at a discount permanently lowers the ceiling of the
  whole book, because that member sets the reference price for every referral they send.
