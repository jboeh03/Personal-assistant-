# Pricing

**Owner:** `wpc-strategist` · **Status:** the only source of truth for prices in this project

**Every price in Westside Property Care 513 appears on this page. If a number appears
anywhere else in this repository and disagrees with this file, this file wins and the other
document is wrong.** Never invent a price. Quote this page verbatim.

> **Rewritten 2026-08-05** to the owner's signed service agreement. The membership is **one
> flat price** billed across an **eight-month season**. The four attribute-based
> configurations are dead and may not appear anywhere outside `_source/` and the marked

## 1. The membership — one number

# **$279 per month**

That is the entire membership price list. There is no tier, no module, no add-on price, and
no configuration table. **A property with a pool and an outdoor kitchen pays exactly the same
as a property with neither.**

| What it covers | |
|---|---|
| **Price** | **$279 per month** |
| **Billed** | Monthly, during the **March 1 – October 31** service season — **8 charges a season** |
| **Includes** | **2 scheduled property-care visits per month**, **16 per season** |
| **Each visit** | **Up to 2 hours of on-site service.** Unused service time does not roll over. |
| **Not billed** | November 1 – end of February. No charge, and no scheduled visits. |

**Season total per client: $279 × 8 = $2,232.**

### Why property attributes no longer change the price

They still change the *work* — a pool adds roughly thirty minutes to a visit and an outdoor
kitchen roughly twenty (`unit-economics.md` §2.1). Under a flat price that difference is
absorbed by **the two-hour cap and by client selection**, not by the invoice. Pool and outdoor
kitchen now decide **whether he takes the client**, not what he charges them.
`ideal-client.md` is where that logic lives, and it is not optional — it is what makes one
price survivable across properties of very different size.

### How to say it

> *"It's $279 a month, March through October. Two visits a month, up to two hours each. Same
> number whether you've got a pool or you don't."*

Then move immediately to what stays maintained. **Do not put the price and the hours in the
same breath.** `$279 ÷ 4 hours = $69.75`, which lands inside the Cincinnati handyman band and
reframes a maintenance membership as four hours of general labour. The two-hour cap is a
**scope boundary** and belongs where boundaries belong — in the agreement and on the
what's-included page — not next to the price. `unit-economics.md` §9.4 has the arithmetic;
`CANON.md` §3 makes it a rule.

### What $279 is not

- **It is not $249.** $249 appeared on an early flyer draft and on the work-block plan. It is
  **withdrawn.** The document a customer signs says $279, and that is the price.
  *($249 does remain a **project** price — the grill and outdoor kitchen deep clean at $249+
  in §5.1. It is not, and never was, the membership price. See the note in §5.1.)*
- **It is not four numbers.** Any surviving reference to attribute-based configurations is
  from the superseded model and is wrong.
- **It is not twelve months.** Eight. Anything multiplying $279 by 12 is an error.

## 2. Founding Member rate

**The first 4 clients lock $279 for the remainder of the 2026 season and all of the 2027
season.**

- The lock covers the **membership price only** — not project work and not materials.
- It runs through **October 31, 2027**. It does not extend into 2028.
- It is a real commitment and it is stated in the service agreement. It is also the strongest
  close available before there is a portfolio of finished work to point at: he is asking the
  first four people to take a chance on a business with no track record, and a locked price
  through a full season is a fair thing to give them for it.
- **After four members, the Founding rate is gone.** Members 5 and 6 pay the standard rate on
  standard terms. Do not extend it quietly to fill the last two slots — see §8.

**What the lock is really buying, and he should know this.** In a seasonal business the
Founding lock is not primarily a discount. It is a **re-enrolment device**: it gives the
person who signs in August 2026 a stated, written reason to come back on March 1, 2027, at a
moment when four months of silence have gone by. That is the single biggest risk in the model
(`decisions.md` **D-18(b)**), and the lock is the cheapest available answer to it.

## 3. Season, pro-ration, and the four months with no bill

### 3.1 The season

**March 1 – October 31.** Eight billed months. Sixteen visits.

**November 1 – end of February: no charge and no scheduled visits.** Say this plainly on the
walkthrough and put it in the agreement. A member who is surprised by it in November is a
member who does not come back in March.

There is a **separate, optional winter offering** (`03-marketing/winter-service.md`). It is
**not part of this membership**, it is not included, it is not priced here, and it must never
be described as though it were.

### 3.2 Pro-ration — by visit

**A partial month is billed by the visit.**

```
$279 ÷ 2 scheduled visits = $139.50 per visit
```

A client whose service starts mid-month and receives one visit that month is billed **$139.50
for that month**, then full months thereafter. The same rule runs in reverse for a member who
starts a cancellation notice mid-season.

**Worked example — a client starting mid-August 2026:**

```
August      1 visit   ×  $139.50   =   $139.50
September   2 visits              =   $279.00
October     2 visits              =   $279.00
                                      -------
2026 total  5 visits              =   $697.50
Check: 5 × $139.50 = $697.50  ✓
```

**Why by visit and not by calendar day:** it needs no arithmetic in a driveway, it is
obviously fair in both directions, and it matches what the customer actually received. A
calendar-day pro-ration would charge a client for eleven days in which nobody came.

*(This rule is `wpc-strategist`'s, not the owner's — see `decisions.md` **R-7**. Confirm it
with him before the first agreement is signed.)*

### 3.3 There is no winter rate, because there is no winter service

**Never propose a reduced winter rate, a winter pause, or a twelve-month spread of the
seasonal price.** All three create the same problem: they blur the boundary of what was sold,
and the boundary is the product.

The superseded model argued *"the same price in January as in July."* **That argument is dead
and must not survive anywhere.** What replaced it is in `CANON.md` §4: sixteen visits, six
properties, the same person every time, who writes down what he saw.

## 4. Billing terms

| Term | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Payment method** | Card on file. Collected before the first visit. No exceptions. |
| **Charge date** | Automatically on the **1st of each month, in advance**, March through October. |
| **Charges per season** | **8.** No charge is raised in November, December, January, or February. |
| **Billing period** | Calendar month. Two scheduled visits fall within it. |
| **Pro-ration** | By visit — $139.50. See §3.2. |
| **Failed card** | Service continues through the current month. Card must be replaced before the next scheduled route day. Past-due accounts may have scheduled service suspended until current (agreement §11). |
| **Cancellation** | **30 days' written notice**, either party. Amounts earned for completed service periods remain due. Founding Members may cancel on the same terms — the lock is a price guarantee, not a term commitment. |
| **Off-season** | The card stays on file. It is not charged. Re-enrolment for the next season is confirmed in writing before March 1. |
| **Projects** | Invoiced on completion, charged to the card on file unless the member arranges otherwise. |
| **Materials** | Itemised on the estimate and on the invoice, never folded into labour. See §6. |

**Why card-on-file is non-negotiable:** a six-client business cannot spend any part of its
month chasing $279. It is also a qualification signal — see `ideal-client.md` §6.5.

**The off-season row is the one people skip, and it is the one that matters.** The card stays
on file, unused, for four months. That is a stored payment method attached to a relationship
with no touchpoints, which is exactly the condition under which people call their bank rather
than their contractor. The re-enrolment conversation happens in **February, in writing, before
the first March route day** — not on the day he shows up.

## 6. Materials

**Materials are billed separately at cost + 25%, itemised.**

- Every material line appears on the estimate before the work starts and on the invoice after.
- Materials never disappear into labour revenue. If a project consumes $75 of materials, the
  client sees $75 + $18.75 = **$93.75**, and the books show it as its own line.
- The 25% covers sourcing, the supply trip, and carrying the cost until the invoice clears.
  It is not padding and it does not need to be apologised for.
- Disposal and dump fees are treated as materials: at cost + 25%.
- **Pool chemicals, salt, filters, and cartridges are materials, not membership items** —
  agreement §7 says so, and `service-catalog.md` §4.1 excludes them from the membership.
- Routine consumables used on a membership visit — surface cleaner, degreaser, microfibre,
  trash bags, spot weed treatment — are **not** materials. They are covered by the membership
  and budgeted as an operating expense (`unit-economics.md` A21).

## 7. How to quote a project

The point of this section is that he never has to guess with a customer standing next to him.

### The formula

```
  estimated on-site hours  ×  $100
+ materials at cost        ×  1.25
= subtotal, rounded UP to the nearest $25
```

**Floor: no standalone trip is quoted below $149.** A dedicated drive, setup, and teardown
costs the same whether the job is twenty minutes or ninety.

**The floor does not apply to work performed during a scheduled membership visit.** He is
already on the property and already paid to be there, so the trip is free. That is exactly why
the $49 window well works and why it would be a bad standalone job.

**One addition under the two-hour cap:** work performed during a visit still has to be
*additional to* the two hours, not carved out of them. If a $49 window well is done inside the
two hours, the membership paid for it and the project revenue is fictional. Name it, price it,
and do it **after** the routine visit or on a separate trip — see `operating-model.md` §8,
membership standard 4.

### Check the formula against the published prices

| Job | On-site hours | Calculation | Result | Published |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grill & outdoor kitchen deep clean | 2.5 | 2.5 × $100 = $250 | $250 | **$249+** ✓ |
| Window well cleanout, during a visit | 0.5 | 0.5 × $100 = $50 | $50 | **$49+** ✓ |
| Patio pressure wash | 2.5 | 2.5 × $100 = $250, round up | $250 | from $249 ✓ |
| Driveway + patio | 4.5 | 4.5 × $100 = $450 | $450 | from $449 ✓ |

The formula reproduces the published prices. That is the point — the price list and the
quoting rule are the same system, so a quoted job never lands below a published one.

### Why $100 an hour and not less

Cincinnati handyman labour runs **$40–80/hour**. He is not selling handyman hours. He is
selling a flat number for a finished result, with his own equipment, from someone the client
already trusts and already lets through the gate. That combination prices above hourly labour,
not at it. If a job pencils out below $80/hour of on-site time, the estimate is wrong — either
the hours are underestimated or the job shouldn't be taken.

### The five rules of quoting

1. **Never quote a flat number in the driveway from memory.** Walk it, measure it, photograph
   it. Send the number the same day or the next morning.
2. **If pressed for a number on the spot, give a range** — and set the low end where you'd
   still be glad to do the work. The final number lands inside the range you said.
3. **Quote a flat price, never an hourly rate.** An hourly rate invites the client to watch
   the clock and turns every efficiency he's earned into a discount he gives away.
4. **Materials are always a separate line.** Never bundle them to make a number look rounder.
5. **Underestimate the hours once and you've set the price for that job forever.** If the
   first pressure wash takes four hours and you quoted 2.5, the number to fix is next
   season's quote, not this season's invoice.

## 8. What is never discounted

- **The membership price.** Someone who negotiates $279 before the first visit will negotiate
  the scope after it. See `ideal-client.md` §6.3.
- **The last slot.** Filling slot 6 at a discount permanently lowers the ceiling of the whole
  book, because that member sets the reference price for every referral they send.
- **Project work for members.** See §5.3 — they get priority and no trip minimum instead.
- **Re-enrolment.** A returning member pays $279. Do not invent a loyalty rate; the answer to
  a member who hesitates in February is a **conversation**, not a discount.

**If a closing concession is genuinely needed**, give **scheduling priority**, a **free first
project**, or an **extra visit in the first month** — never a lower monthly number. A price
concession is permanent and visible to every referral; a one-time concession is neither.
