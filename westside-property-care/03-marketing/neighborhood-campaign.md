## 1. What this track is for, and what it is not for

`brand-brief.md` §7 states the job of a door hanger and a yard sign in one line: **make a
qualified neighbor recognize the name later and know it is nearby.** Not sell. Nobody puts a
$279/month card on file because of something left on a doorknob. This track's actual output is
route compactness — every qualified lead it produces is, by construction, near an existing
client or near home base, which is the profit lever this whole business runs on
(`operating-model.md` §4).

Two things follow from that:

1. **Low print volumes, high targeting precision.** This is not a saturation campaign. There are
   a few hundred qualifying homes across the entire target geography (`ideal-client.md` §3); a
   single pass of 150–250 pieces covers a meaningful fraction of a cluster's immediate radius.
2. **The conversation, not the paper, does the qualifying.** Every asset in this track exists to
   get one of two outcomes: a call or text to `[PHONE]`, or a name added to the waitlist because
   a neighbor mentioned it. The actual qualification — a pool, an outdoor kitchen, or both;
   already outsources maintenance; the time-fit test; the route tests
   (`ideal-client.md` §2.2) — happens on the phone and at the walkthrough
   (`ideal-client.md` §7), never on the printed piece.

## 2. Geography — qualified only

### 2.1 The qualified areas, and what "qualified" means for street selection

Per `ideal-client.md` §3, verbatim:

| Area | Note for street selection |
|---|---|
| **Green Township subdivisions** | The core target. Green Township is made up of several named communities, including Bridgetown, Covedale, Dent, Mack, Monfort Heights, and White Oak — Bridgetown is **conditional**, see below. Newer subdivisions here (for example, developments in the northwest part of the township) carry the highest density of in-ground pools and built outdoor kitchens on the west side; prioritize these first. |
| **Oak Hills** | Established, well-kept streets with mature outdoor investment — look for older in-ground pools and additions, not new construction. |
| **Upper Delhi** | The top of Delhi's $180–350k range only — river-side and higher-value streets. Do not treat Delhi generally as qualified; most of it is not. |
| **Covedale** | Well-maintained pockets with owner-occupied homes above the west-side median, including newer empty-nester developments. |

**Bridgetown is conditional**, not qualified by address alone (`ideal-client.md` §3). It sits
inside Green Township and averages ~$240k, at the bottom of the qualified range. A Bridgetown
street only gets a door-hanger pass if a specific property on it clears the attribute test first
(a visible pool or outdoor kitchen investment) — it does not get a blanket drop the way a Green
Township subdivision at $250–450k does.

### 2.2 Explicitly excluded — do not target, full stop

**Westwood. Price Hill. Cheviot.** No door hangers, no flyers, no yard signs, no leave-behinds,
no follow-up cards, on any street in these areas, ever, under this campaign. `ideal-client.md`
§3 is arithmetic, not a judgment about the neighborhoods: at a ~$175k median, a $279/month
membership addresses a fraction of a percent of the households there, and this business only
needs six clients total. If a qualifying inquiry comes in *from* one of these areas unprompted —
someone who heard about him some other way and genuinely clears the attribute and route tests —
take the walkthrough. **An exception is fine. A campaign is not.**

### 2.3 How to actually find the streets, without fabricating a list

No agent working on this file has street-level data on which specific houses have pools or
outdoor kitchens, and inventing one would be worse than not having one. This is the method to
build the real list, in order — the same method `first-20-prospects.md` uses to build the first
target list:

1. **Hamilton County Auditor property records** (publicly searchable) list improvements on a
   parcel, and in-ground pools are frequently recorded there as an assessed feature. This is the
   single best desk-research starting point for identifying candidate streets inside the
   qualified subdivisions before ever driving them.
2. **Satellite and street-level map imagery** of the qualified subdivisions — pools are visible
   from above, and outdoor kitchens, covered patios, and built-in grills are often visible from
   the street. This confirms or rules out what the auditor records suggest.
3. **The actual drive or walk.** Nothing replaces seeing the property in person — a pool cover
   in April, a stone outdoor kitchen counter, a well-kept patio set are all visible from the curb
   and none of them show up in records.
4. **The TSGC warm list, if confirmed** (`warm-list-campaign.md`). Every qualified-geography
   address on that list is a pre-identified target street even before Track B does any of its
   own research — fold those addresses into the street list first.
5. **Once client 1 signs:** the single best source of new target streets becomes the streets
   immediately around that property. This is the "adjacent to existing clients" rule in §3.

### 2.4 Priority order for street selection

1. Streets within a few minutes of an existing signed client (once one exists) — this is the
   compactness rule in `operating-model.md` §4 applied to prospecting, not just to acceptance.
2. Streets within a few minutes of home base, inside the qualified subdivisions, before any
   client is signed — this is where client 1 and client 2 get selected from, and
   `ideal-client.md` §4 says to pick them for cluster potential, not just for being the first
   yes.
3. Any other qualified-subdivision street with a visible pool or outdoor kitchen, once the above
   two are covered.

**Never:** a street outside the qualified geography, regardless of how many pools are visible on
it. §2.2 has no exceptions for attractive-looking streets in excluded areas.

## 3. The referral mechanic

`ideal-client.md` §2.4 calls a neighbor of an existing member "the single best lead type in the
business," and §4 turns a waitlisted, off-route prospect into a pipeline by asking them who else
on their street qualifies. This campaign operationalizes both.

### 3.1 The ask, on the service report or in person

This is not a paid referral program. There is no bounty, no discount, no credit —
`pricing.md` §8 never discounts the membership, and inventing an incentive mechanic that isn't
budgeted anywhere in `unit-economics.md` would be exactly the kind of number this project
doesn't invent. What it costs nothing to do is ask, directly:

> Do you know anyone else on the street with a pool or an outdoor kitchen? I've got
> {{slots_open}} of the six spots open and I'd rather work streets I'm already on.

Said in person at the end of a visit, or written into a service report as a single line, this
does two things a print piece can't: it names the actual mechanism (fewer trips, more time per
stop), and it comes from someone the neighbor is about to hear about anyway.

### 3.2 With permission, use the name

`messaging.md` §6 — a neighbor referral leads with the referring member's actual name, if they
gave permission, then the route framing: "you're two streets from someone I already take care
of." Never name a client without asking first. Never put a client's name or address on a printed
asset (`voice.md` mechanics table — street name only, never a full address, never in a way that
identifies a specific house).

### 3.3 The yard sign is part of this mechanic, not separate from it

`assets/yard-sign.md` is designed to run at a consenting existing client's home specifically
because it turns the referral mechanic into something visible to the whole street, continuously,
at zero incremental cost per lead. See that file for the copy and the permission requirement.

## 4. Sequence and timing

### 4.1 Before client 1 signs

- Build the initial street list (§2.3, steps 1–3) inside the qualified subdivisions nearest home
  base. `first-20-prospects.md` is this step's worksheet.
- Distribute the first door-hanger pass (§5) — this is also client-1 and client-2 prospecting,
  so weight it toward subdivisions with the highest visible density of pools and outdoor
  kitchens, per `ideal-client.md` §4's instruction to pick the anchor clients for cluster
  potential.
- No yard sign yet — there's no consenting client's property to place it at.

### 4.2 Once client 1 (and ideally client 2) signs

- Re-center the street list on the new client(s)' immediate radius, per §2.4's priority order.
- Ask permission for a yard sign at the client's property (`assets/yard-sign.md` §"Placement and
  permission").
- Start using the leave-behind (`assets/leave-behind.md`) and the flyer (`assets/flyer.md`) at
  every qualifying conversation from here forward — once there's a real client, the walkthrough
  conversation has something concrete to point at even without photography yet.

### 4.3 Ongoing, through the founding four and into slots 5–6

- Door-hanger passes continue at the pace `gtm-plan.md` §4 sets, always re-centered on the
  current route clusters, never widened back out to the full qualified geography once clusters
  exist.
- Follow-up cards (`assets/follow-up-card.md`) go out roughly 10–14 days after a door-hanger
  drop with no response, and after any qualifying conversation that didn't close on the spot.
- **August through October is the priority window** (`gtm-plan.md` §2.5) — properties show a
  full season's wear and the conversation is easiest to demonstrate, not just describe. Weight
  passes toward this window rather than spreading them evenly across the calendar.
- The referral ask (§3.1) happens at every visit, indefinitely — it's free and it never stops,
  even after the book is full, because it's what refills a cancelled slot later
  (`gtm-plan.md` §5).

### 4.4 When this stops

Per `gtm-plan.md` §5: **the moment slot 6 is filled, all distribution under this track stops.**
No more door hangers or flyers print, no more passes happen. The yard sign at an existing
client's home can stay up as a passive credibility signal (it's not generating demand he can't
serve — it's already sitting at a house he already services), but nothing active continues. The
referral ask in §3.1 is the one exception, because it feeds the waitlist rather than an open
slot.

**November through February, this track is dormant regardless of how many slots are open**
(`gtm-plan.md` §2.5). It is not a prospecting season — it's reserved for the off-season touches
in `operating-model.md` §3.2 and, if approved, the paid work in `winter-service.md`. Distribution
resumes in March with the new season, not before.

## 5. Distribution mechanics

- **Print in small batches — 150 to 250 door hangers per pass, not a bulk run.** The open-slot
  count on the asset changes every time a member signs, and printing thousands up front either
  goes stale or tempts someone to leave a fake number on it, which `voice.md` bans outright
  (§6.5 — the real count, never a manufactured one).
- **Walk, don't mail, for the door hanger.** Hand-delivery to a targeted street costs nothing but
  time and keeps the whole campaign inside the "no advertising budget" model. Reserve postage
  for the follow-up card, where a personal, addressed touch is worth the stamp (see
  `assets/follow-up-card.md`).
- **Time the walk for daylight, and preferably a weekday afternoon** when a resident is more
  likely to be arriving home and might see him in person — a five-minute in-person conversation
  converts better than any printed piece and costs nothing extra once he's already on the
  street. When a conversation happens on the spot, the flyer (`assets/flyer.md`) is what he
  hands over — it explains everything in ten seconds without requiring the full leave-behind.
- **Never drop on a street mid-walk without finishing the block.** A partial pass on a
  qualifying street looks like a fluke; a complete pass looks deliberate, and deliberate is the
  entire brand argument.

## 6. What each asset in this track is for

| Asset | Moment it's used | Its one job |
|---|---|---|
| `assets/door-hanger.md` | Cold drop on a targeted street, nobody home | Recognition. No price, no pitch — just the name and the real open-slot count. |
| `assets/flyer.md` | Handed over on the spot in a brief doorstep conversation, or left with someone who wants "something to look at" | The whole offer in ten seconds — what it is, what it costs, what's in it, how to reach him. The general-purpose piece. |
| `assets/yard-sign.md` | Standing, at a consenting existing client's home | Passive proof to the whole street that he's already nearby. |
| `assets/leave-behind.md` | Handed over during a longer qualifying conversation or a walkthrough | The full offer, including the exclusions in detail, in the order `messaging.md` §3 requires. |
| `assets/follow-up-card.md` | 10–14 days after a drop or an unclosed conversation | A second, warmer touch. Still no pressure — the real count and the door stays open. |

The flyer and the leave-behind are not redundant. The flyer is the piece that fits a five-minute
doorstep exchange or a hand-off with no conversation at all — it has to work standing alone. The
leave-behind is the piece used when there's genuinely time to walk the exclusions before the
price, which `ideal-client.md` §7 says should happen before anyone signs anything.
