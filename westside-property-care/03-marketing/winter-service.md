## 1. Why this exists — and it's retention, not revenue

Say this plainly, because it's the entire reason to build this instead of leaving the off-season
alone.

**He signs members in August 2026.** Under his own calendar, an August signer gets three visits
— one pro-rated visit in August, one round each in September and October — and then **four
months of silence before the March 1 route day.** `operating-model.md` §7.3 names this exact
cohort as the highest-risk group in the business, and `decisions.md` D-18(b) is blunt about what
that silence costs: a seasonal membership requires an *active decision to come back*, where an
annual one only requires an active decision to leave, and the industry evidence is that
seasonal-billing operators retain 10–20% fewer customers than annual-contract ones for exactly
this reason — customers who feel undervalued, and customers who **simply forget to renew between
seasons.**

`operating-model.md` §3.2 already answers part of this with two free, unbilled written touches —
a season-end report in late November and a short note in mid-January — plus a February
re-enrollment confirmation. Those stay, regardless of whether this proposal is ever approved.
**What this file proposes is additive: real, paid, in-person labor that gives the relationship
something to point at besides a note in an inbox**, during exactly the four months when a
card-on-file with no visits attached is at its most vulnerable to a phone call to the bank
instead of to him.

**The revenue is not the point, and the numbers below prove it.** At the scope and price
proposed in §4, this offering is worth a few hundred dollars per member across four months — a
rounding error next to the $2,232 membership season. Its job is to be the thing that makes the
March 1 re-enrollment number in `operating-model.md` §7.3 (target: 6 of 6) actually hold.

## 2. Proposed scope

Two structural rules constrain everything below, both inherited directly from decisions already
locked elsewhere in this project — this proposal does not invent new boundaries, it applies the
existing ones to a new season:

- **Not a snow contractor.** No driveway or sidewalk snow or ice clearing, ever — this is
  already an established boundary (`assets/leave-behind.md`'s own copy: "not a handyman, not a
  snow contractor, not an emergency service"). Ice management under this proposal is confined to
  the same outdoor living area the membership covers — the patio, the walkway serving it, the
  pool deck — never the driveway and never the public sidewalk.
- **No ladder, no roof, above roof line.** Same rule as `service-catalog.md` §4.4. Gutter and
  downspout work under this proposal is ground-level clearing and a discharge check, same as the
  membership's own gutter boundary — not a from-the-ladder cleaning. Full ladder-based gutter
  cleaning is a separate project, quoted after he looks at it. It is not a published price —
  `pricing.md` §5.3 lists single-story and two-story figures as **internal quoting anchors only**,
  and `voice.md` §3's anchor-leak rule names "gutters from $179" as its own literal example of a
  banned leak. It stays available as an upsell, quoted separately after an on-site look, not
  folded into this package and never quoted as a range or a starting price.
- **No pool work of any kind.** The task list this proposal is built from doesn't include the
  pool, and that's deliberate — it keeps this offering out of the insurance question already
  open for the membership's pool scope (`CANON.md` §10.3) and out of the pool-company comparison
  entirely. Pool opening, closing, and winterization remain referred out, exactly as they are
  under the membership (`service-catalog.md` §4.1).

### The six included items, from the owner's own list

1. **Gutters and downspouts** — cleared of accessible debris from the ground, downspout
   discharge checked and redirected if it's dumping against the foundation.
2. **Freeze protection** — exposed hose bibs fitted with foam covers, garden hoses disconnected
   and drained where present. No plumbing altered, no fixture removed or installed — covering
   and disconnecting only, the same boundary the membership itself draws around fixed elements
   of the property (`service-catalog.md` §4.9).
3. **Storm debris** — hand-collected leaves, sticks, and wind debris from the patio, walkway,
   and pool deck.
4. **Furniture covering and storage** — cushions and furniture covered or moved into a shed or
   garage if accessible; the grill covered.
5. **Hardscape ice management** — a pet-safe ice-melt product applied to the patio, walkway, and
   steps serving the outdoor living area only — never the driveway, never the public sidewalk.
6. **Exterior checks with photos** — a full walk of the outdoor living area each visit, before
   and after photos, and a written note of anything observed (a cracked paver, a fence panel
   that's come loose, a downspout that needs a real repair) — the same "communicate anything
   unusual" standard that governs every membership visit (`operating-model.md` §8, standard 2).

### Proposed structure — two visits, not a recurring program

Given the scope above, the strongest structural fit is **two visits across the off-season**, not
a recurring monthly service and not a single visit:

| Visit | Timing | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Winter Prep** | Mid-to-late November, near the true season close | All six items above, more thoroughly — this is the closing-out visit |
| **Winter Check** | Mid-to-late January | A lighter re-check: gutters/downspouts, debris, furniture/cover check, ice-melt top-up if needed, exterior photos |

**This timing is not a coincidence — it deliberately lines up with the two free written touches
already required in `operating-model.md` §3.2** (late November, mid-January). For a member who
buys this package, the paid visit *is* the delivery mechanism for that touch — he's already
there doing real work, so the written note that follows is richer than a note written from
memory. For a member who doesn't buy it, the two free written touches still happen exactly as
`operating-model.md` §3.2 already specifies, unaffected by whether this proposal is ever
approved.

**A lighter, single-visit option is also worth having on the table** for a member who wants one
touch, not two — see §4.2.

## 3. What this is not, stated as plainly as the membership's own exclusions

- **Not part of the membership agreement.** It is never bundled into $279, never described as
  "year-round protection," and never offered as a way to soften the March 1 – October 31
  boundary. `CANON.md` §3 and `voice.md` §6.4 both ban this framing outright, and this proposal
  does not ask for an exception.
- **Not available to fill a seventh membership slot.** Buying this does not extend, replace, or
  substitute for a membership, and it carries no route-hour cap interaction — it's ordinary
  off-season project work, the same category as a winter grill deep clean, and `gtm-plan.md` §5
  already treats project work as uncapped.
- **Not a snow contract, not pool work, not ladder work** — see §2.
- **Not recurring, not a subscription.** Each visit is a flat-priced project, invoiced like any
  other project (`pricing.md` §4), not a card charged automatically on a schedule.

## 4. Proposed pricing — built from `pricing.md` §7's own formula

```
  estimated on-site hours  ×  $100
+ materials at cost        ×  1.25
= subtotal, rounded UP to the nearest $25
Floor: no standalone trip quoted below $149 (this is always a standalone trip — never during
a scheduled membership visit, since there are none in the off-season).
```

### 4.1 Recommended — the two-visit package

**Winter Prep visit** (all six items, the more thorough of the two):

```
On-site time: ~2.0 hrs (arrival/walkthrough 15 min, furniture covering/storage 25 min,
storm debris 20 min, gutters/downspouts ground-level 15 min, freeze protection 15 min,
initial ice-melt application 10 min, final walk + photos + report 10 min)

2.0 hrs × $100                    = $200.00
Materials (ice-melt, foam bib
covers, misc.) ~$30 × 1.25        =  $37.50
                                     -------
Subtotal                          = $237.50
Rounded up to nearest $25         = $250
```

**Winter Check visit** (lighter re-check):

```
On-site time: ~1.25 hrs (arrival/walkthrough 10 min, gutters/downspouts re-check 15 min,
storm debris 15 min, furniture/cover check 15 min, ice-melt top-up 10 min, final walk +
photos 10 min)

1.25 hrs × $100                   = $125.00
Materials (ice-melt top-up)
~$15 × 1.25                       =  $18.75
                                     -------
Subtotal                          = $143.75
Rounded up to nearest $25         = $150 (clears the $149 floor)
```

**Proposed package price: $250 + $150 = $400 total, billed once as a flat project — not
monthly, not a membership fee.**

### 4.2 Lighter option — a single combined visit

For a member who wants one touch instead of two — a later signer, or someone who only wants the
essentials:

```
On-site time: ~2.25 hrs (a condensed version of all six items in one visit)

2.25 hrs × $100                   = $225.00
Materials ~$40 × 1.25             =  $50.00
                                     -------
Subtotal                          = $275.00
Rounded up to nearest $25         = $275 (already a multiple of $25)
```

**Proposed single-visit price: $275.**

### 4.3 Why this is clearly cheaper and lighter than the membership — the check the formula itself demands

`pricing.md` §7 checks its own formula against the published prices to prove the two systems
agree; the same check applies here, in the other direction — proving this proposal cannot be
mistaken for a way to buy the membership on the cheap:

| | Membership (full season) | Winter package (proposed) |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $2,232 (`pricing.md` §3.1) | $400 (two visits) or $275 (one visit) |
| As a share of the membership | — | **17.9%** (two-visit) / **12.3%** (one-visit) |
| Visits | 16 | 2, or 1 |
| On-site hours | Up to 32 (at the 2-hr ceiling) | 3.25, or 2.25 |
| Scope | Full outdoor living area, including pool care | Six named items, no pool, ground-level only |

**This table is internal only and must never be copied onto a customer-facing surface as-is.**
Putting the membership's season price and its season hours in adjacent cells is exactly the
value-comparison shape `voice.md` §8 item 36 bans — divide $2,232 by 32 hours and the table hands
a reader (or a future editor lifting a row from it) the hourly-rate framing the hours-arithmetic
rule exists to prevent. It exists here to prove the winter package can't be mistaken for a
cut-rate membership, not as a draft for any page, flyer, or listing.

At roughly a sixth to an eighth of the membership's season price and a fraction of its scope,
this cannot read as a way to sell around the season boundary — it reads as what it is: a small,
optional, clearly bounded add-on.

## 5. Who it's for, and how it would be offered — if approved

**Primary audience: current members**, offered at the final visit of the season
(`voice.md` pair 11 already routes off-season project work generically at that moment — "the
cover, a grill deep clean before the holidays, pressure washing while the deck is empty — that's
project work and I do it year round"; this proposal, if approved, becomes one more named item in
that same conversation).

**Secondary audience: any qualified-geography homeowner**, member or not — ordinary project work
isn't limited to members (`gtm-plan.md` §5), and a non-member who wants gutters cleared and
furniture covered before winter is a perfectly good project customer, and a warm lead for the
membership come March.

**Never pitched as a way around the season boundary.** If a prospect balks at "no visits
November through February" during the qualifying conversation (`ideal-client.md` §5), this
offering is not the answer to that objection — `ideal-client.md` §5, point 3 is explicit that
the winter offering "may not be used to soften it." It gets mentioned only after the season
boundary has already been accepted, not as a rebuttal to it.

## 7. Open questions for the owner — flag, don't guess

1. **Approve, adjust, or reject the scope in §2.** In particular: is ground-level-only gutter
   work sufficient, or does he want to fold in the ladder-based cleaning at the existing
   published rate ($179/$249) as a bundled upgrade rather than a separate upsell?
2. **Approve or adjust the price in §4.** The hour estimates are this document's own, not his —
   confirm they match how long he'd actually expect these tasks to take on a typical property.
3. **Insurance.** Disconnecting hoses and installing freeze covers is light, non-invasive work,
   but it's still work on the property in the off-season under a policy that — per
   `CANON.md` §10.3 — already has one open question about the pool scope. Confirm this scope is
   covered before it's ever sold, not after the first freeze-cover install.
4. **Bandwidth.** `unit-economics.md` §4.2 already expects 2026 to be a thin launch year. Selling
   and delivering a new offering in the same season he's trying to sign his first members may not
   be the right sequencing — it may make more sense to launch this in the 2027–2028 off-season,
   once the founding four are seated and the membership rhythm is established. That's his call,
   not a marketing call.
