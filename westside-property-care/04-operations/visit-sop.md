# Visit SOP

**Owner:** `wpc-ops` · **Status:** field procedure — read this on the truck, not at a desk

Built for one-handed use on a phone, in a driveway, possibly in the rain. Checklists, not
paragraphs. If a step can't be followed while holding a pool net in the other hand, it's
written wrong — flag it.

**No step here is timed, and none of these checklists is a race.** `CANON.md` §3 forbids
"minutes per visit" anywhere, internal or external, and membership standard 1 is *never rush
a property because another client is waiting* (`operating-model.md` §7). Work the list at
the pace the property needs. If a property needs two hours today, it gets two hours.

Every visit has the same four-part shape. What changes between **Green Season** (April
through October) and **Dormant Season** (November through March) is the work in the middle —
see `service-catalog.md` §1: **not every task happens every visit; work is prioritized to
what the property needs that day.**

1. Before arrival
2. Arrival walkaround
3. The work
4. Before leaving

---

## GREEN SEASON — April through October

*The property is in use. The work is about keeping it ready to enjoy.*

### 1. Before arrival

- [ ] Pull up the property file — access notes, pets, gate code, known issues, anything
      flagged from the last visit
- [ ] Confirm access: gate code, key, or that someone will be home
- [ ] Check the weather. If it's a washout, this is a reschedule call, not a visit — see
      `service-report-templates.md` (weather-rescheduled template) and move the visit within
      the same calendar month
- [ ] Load the truck: pool net and pole (if the property has a pool), skimmer/pump basket
      tool, blower, hand tools, trash bags, surface cleaner, microfiber cloths, degreaser for
      the grease trap, spot weed treatment, watering can or hose adapter, phone charged for
      photos

### 2. Arrival walkaround — before touching anything

- [ ] Walk the entire outdoor living area first. Nothing gets cleaned before it's been seen.
- [ ] Look for: storm damage, standing water, fallen limbs, drainage problems, anything off
      with the pool, anything off with the outdoor kitchen, furniture damage, anything
      visibly out of place
- [ ] Photograph anything that deserves documentation — before photos are what make the
      after photos mean something
- [ ] Check the gate latch and note if it's not catching cleanly

### 3. The work — prioritized to what the property needs today

**Not a checklist to complete. A menu of what's usually relevant — do what today's visit
actually calls for.**

**Pool area** *(if the property has a pool)*
- [ ] Skim the surface
- [ ] Empty the skimmer basket
- [ ] Empty the pump basket
- [ ] Clear debris from the pool deck and surround
- [ ] Straighten and wipe pool furniture

**Patio and outdoor living area**
- [ ] Wipe down patio furniture and table surfaces
- [ ] Straighten and reset furniture and cushions
- [ ] Blow hard surfaces clean — patio, walkways, pool deck, outdoor kitchen area
- [ ] General tidy of the outdoor living area

**Outdoor kitchen** *(if the property has one)*
- [ ] Wipe down outdoor countertops
- [ ] Wipe down stainless steel surfaces
- [ ] Empty the flat-top or grill grease trap and drip tray
- [ ] Clear debris from around and under the cooking area

**Grounds within the outdoor living area**
- [ ] Hand-collect loose leaves, sticks, and debris
- [ ] Empty into the homeowner's designated containers, up to their capacity — not beyond it
- [ ] Water flower pots and container plantings
- [ ] Spot-treat visible weeds in hardscape joints, walkway cracks, and along bed edges —
      never inside the beds

**If something is found outside this scope:** stop, don't do it. Name it, locate it, price
it (or say you'd need to look closer), and save it for the report — see
`service-report-templates.md`. Never absorb a project into the visit "since I'm here."

### 4. Before leaving

- [ ] Second walkthrough of the whole outdoor living area
- [ ] Confirm gates are latched
- [ ] Confirm every tool and every piece of trash came off the property with you
- [ ] After photos — anything you photographed on arrival, and anything worth showing
      finished
- [ ] Write down, on the spot, anything that belongs in tonight's report or the property
      file — don't trust memory for this by 6pm
- [ ] Send the service report the same day — see `service-report-templates.md`
- [ ] Update the property record's service history row — see `property-record-template.md`

## DORMANT SEASON — November through March

*The property is not in use. The work shifts from presentation to protection. Catching a
problem in December is worth more than anything he does in July — this is the season the
membership actually earns its keep.*

### 1. Before arrival

- [ ] Pull up the property file — access notes, pets, gate code, known issues, last visit's
      flags
- [ ] Confirm access: gate code, key, or that someone will be home
- [ ] Check the weather and the forecast for a hard freeze in the coming days — that changes
      what's urgent on today's visit
- [ ] If it's a washout or the hardscape is unsafe to work on (ice underfoot), this is a
      reschedule call — see `service-report-templates.md`
- [ ] Load the truck: ice melt (the stone-safe product, not the cheap stuff, for flagstone),
      salt spreader or hand scoop, furniture covers, bungee cords or straps, blower, hand
      tools, trash bags, camera/phone charged — this visit generates more photos than any
      other season

### 2. Arrival walkaround — before touching anything

- [ ] Walk the entire outdoor living area first
- [ ] This is the fullest inspection of the year — leaves are down and everything is
      visible. Take it seriously; the March report is built on what gets seen now and on
      every visit between now and then.
- [ ] Look for: storm damage, ice damming, overflow staining at the gutters, standing water
      or pooling at the foundation, anything off with the pool equipment pad, anything off
      with the outdoor kitchen, covers that worked loose in wind, anything visibly out of
      place
- [ ] Photograph broadly — this season's report leans on photos more than any other

### 3. The work — prioritized to what the property needs today

**Water and freeze protection — the highest-value work of the season. Check this first.**
- [ ] Check hose bibs; confirm shut off and drained
- [ ] Disconnect and store hoses
- [ ] Drain and store or cover exposed lines and fixtures
- [ ] Check outdoor kitchen water lines, sinks, and shutoffs
- [ ] Visually check the pool equipment pad for anything obviously wrong
- [ ] Confirm freeze-vulnerable items are covered, drained, or brought in

**Gutters and drainage — check, not clean; ground level only**
- [ ] Visually inspect gutters and downspouts from the ground — never from a ladder, never
      above roof line
- [ ] Confirm downspouts are discharging and extensions are in place
- [ ] Look for overflow staining, ice damming, standing water, pooling at foundations
- [ ] Note anything found for the report — actual gutter cleaning is a quoted project, not a
      membership task

**Storm and debris**
- [ ] Clear storm debris from the outdoor living area, by hand
- [ ] Hand-collect fallen limbs and branches within routine scope — up to container capacity
- [ ] Clear drains, grates, and window wells of surface debris

**Furniture and equipment**
- [ ] Cover, store, or consolidate outdoor furniture and cushions at season transition (first
      Dormant visit)
- [ ] Cover and secure the grill and outdoor kitchen equipment
- [ ] Check that covers from the last visit are still secure — wind moves them

**Ice and hardscape**
- [ ] Salt and clear ice from the outdoor living hardscape — patio, pool deck, and the
      walkway serving it — on this scheduled visit
- [ ] Use the stone-safe product on flagstone or natural stone; cheaper product pits it
- [ ] Note any salt damage to stone or concrete and adjust product on the next visit

**Inspection and spring planning**
- [ ] Full exterior photo inspection — every visit this season, not just once
- [ ] Note and photograph anything worth flagging for spring
- [ ] **March visit only:** the report includes what the property will need in April, with
      prices. This is the highest-converting report of the year — treat it that way, not as
      a formality.

**If something is found outside this scope:** stop, don't do it. Name it, locate it, price
it, and save it for the report. Same rule as Green Season — never absorb a project into the
visit.

### 4. Before leaving

- [ ] Second walkthrough of the whole outdoor living area
- [ ] Confirm gates are latched
- [ ] Confirm every tool and every piece of trash came off the property with you
- [ ] Confirm covers are actually secured, not just draped — check this twice in this season
- [ ] After photos
- [ ] Write down anything that belongs in tonight's report or the property file
- [ ] Send the service report the same day — see `service-report-templates.md`
- [ ] Update the property record's service history row — see `property-record-template.md`
- [ ] **March visit:** confirm the spring-prep pricing is in the report before it goes out,
      not added later

## What never appears on this SOP, on purpose

- **No minutes-per-visit target, anywhere.** `CANON.md` §3, `operating-model.md` §6.
- **No arrival time or arrival window.** Clients are told their route day, not their hour —
  `service-catalog.md` §4.11.
- **No task treated as mandatory on every visit.** The season lists above are what's usually
  relevant, not a checklist that has to be fully checked to count as a complete visit —
  `service-catalog.md` §1.
