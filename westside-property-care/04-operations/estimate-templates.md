# Estimate Templates

**Owner:** `wpc-ops` · **Status:** working templates for the project add-on catalog

This is where a recommendation from a service report (`service-report-templates.md`)
becomes a signed-off, flat-priced job. Every template below is built from the quoting
formula in `00-model/pricing.md` §7 and structured so **a quoted project cannot quietly
grow** — scope and exclusions are named on the estimate itself, not assumed.

---

## The formula (internal — never customer-facing as a formula)

```
  estimated on-site hours  ×  $100
+ materials at cost        ×  1.25
= subtotal, rounded UP to the nearest $25
```

**Floor: no standalone trip is quoted below $149.** A dedicated drive, setup, and teardown
costs the same whether the job takes twenty minutes or ninety.

**The floor does not apply to work added during a scheduled membership visit** — he's
already on the property and already paid to be there. That's why the $49 window well works
as a same-visit add-on and would be a bad standalone job.

## The five rules, every time (from `pricing.md` §7)

1. **Never quote a flat number from memory in the driveway.** Walk it, measure it,
   photograph it. Send the number the same day or the next morning.
2. **If pressed for a number on the spot, give a range** — set the low end where you'd
   still be glad to do the work. The final number lands inside the range you said.
3. **Quote a flat price, never an hourly rate.** An hourly rate invites the client to watch
   the clock.
4. **Materials are always a separate line.** Never bundle them to make the number look
   rounder.
5. **Underestimate the hours once and you've set the price for that job forever.** If it
   runs long, the number to fix is next year's quote — never this year's invoice.

---

## Why every estimate names what's *not* included

An estimate that only lists what's included leaves the boundary to memory, and memory is
exactly what a customer standing in a driveway three weeks later won't share. Every
template below carries three fields, always in this order, and none of them is optional:

1. **What this covers** — the specific job, specific surfaces, specific counts
2. **What this doesn't cover** — named, so a bigger version of the same job is visibly a
   different job
3. **What happens if it's bigger once he's on site** — stop, name what changed, send a
   revised number before doing the additional work. Nothing beyond the estimate gets done
   without a new number agreed first.

That third field is the one that keeps a $449 pressure wash from turning into a $700 job
with no conversation in between.

## The generic shape — use this structure for every estimate

```
[JOB NAME] — [ADDRESS, street name is fine for internal use]

[One line: the flat price.]

That's a flat price, not an hourly rate. If it takes longer than planned, it still costs
$[price].

What this covers:
— [specific item]
— [specific item]
— [specific item]

What this doesn't cover:
— [specific exclusion]
— [specific exclusion]
(Anything not listed above needs its own estimate before it's added.)

Materials are billed separately at cost plus 25% and itemized on the invoice. For this job
I [don't expect any beyond what's already on the truck / expect approximately $__ of
materials — itemized: __]. If that changes once I'm on site, I'll tell you before I start,
not after.

If the job turns out to be bigger than what's described above once I'm working — more
surface, worse buildup, something structural — I'll stop, tell you what changed, and send a
revised number before doing any of the additional work.

Good for 30 days.

[owner name] · [phone] · WPC513
```

## Published-price jobs

These two prices are the only project prices ever shown to a customer before an on-site
look (`pricing.md` §5.1). The "+" means the published number is the floor for the simplest
version of the job — confirm or raise it on site, never lower it.

### Grill and outdoor kitchen deep clean — $249+

```
Grill and outdoor kitchen deep clean — [address]

$249, flat.

That's a flat price, not an hourly rate.

What this covers:
— Burners, grates, and flavorizer bars, cleaned and degreased
— Interior of the grill and the hood
— Full teardown and reassembly

What this doesn't cover:
— A flat-top, a side burner, or stone counters beyond the grill itself — if the property
  has any of those, this is quoted separately and will run above $249; I'll confirm the
  exact number before starting
— Anything requiring a replacement part (see `service-agreement.md` §6.9)

Materials are billed separately at cost plus 25%. For a standard grill I don't expect any
beyond what's on the truck.

If it turns out to be a full outdoor kitchen setup rather than a standard grill once I'm
looking at it, I'll stop and send a revised number before continuing.

Good for 30 days.
```

### Window well cleanout — $49+

*Usually added to a scheduled membership visit, not a standalone trip — the $149 floor
doesn't apply here (`pricing.md` §7).*

```
Window well cleanout, [count] wells, [location] — added to your [date] visit.

$49 per well, flat. [count] wells = $[total].

What this covers:
— Removing leaves, debris, and standing water from the well(s) named above
— Confirming the well drains after clearing

What this doesn't cover:
— Wells not named above
— A well cover repair or replacement, if one is cracked or missing
— Anything found below the well grate that looks structural — that gets flagged, not
  touched, and quoted separately if needed

No materials expected on this job.

Add it to this visit? Say the word.
```

## Quoted categories — no published price, always an on-site look first

These are quoted after walking the job — never estimated from the driveway or from a
description over the phone (`pricing.md` §5.2). Use the generic shape above with the
internal anchors below as your own starting point, not as a number you say out loud before
you've measured anything.

### Pressure washing

```
[Surface(s)], pressure washed — [address]

$[flat price].

What this covers:
— [surface named — e.g., "the driveway and the back patio"]
— One pass, standard surface cleaner

What this doesn't cover:
— Any surface not named above (siding, fence, deck, walkway) — each is its own line if
  wanted, priced separately
— Sealing, staining, or any treatment after washing
— Removal of oil stains, rust staining, or anything requiring a specialty chemical beyond
  the standard cleaner — flagged and quoted separately if found

Materials are billed separately at cost plus 25%. For this job I [don't expect any beyond
what's already on the truck / expect approximately $__].

If the surface needs more than one pass or a specialty treatment once I've started, I'll
stop and send a revised number before continuing.

Good for 30 days.
```

### Storm cleanup beyond routine debris

```
Storm cleanup, [scope] — [address]

$[flat price], or: quoted after inspection if a saw, trailer, or disposal is required.

What this covers:
— Hand-collected debris from [area], one trip, within [container / hauling arrangement]

What this doesn't cover:
— Tree or large-limb removal
— Structural or property damage repair
— Anything requiring more than one trip or more container capacity than described — this
  gets a revised number, not a surprise invoice

Disposal, if any, is billed at cost plus 25%, itemized.

Good for 30 days, or until conditions change materially — storm scope moves fast, so confirm
before scheduling if more than a few days have passed.
```

## The on-the-spot range script

For when a homeowner wants a number before you've walked the job:

> "I'd rather look at it first so the number's right, but if I had to guess before seeing
> it — probably somewhere in the $[low]–$[high] range. I'll confirm the real number
> tomorrow morning once I've actually measured it."

Set the low end where you'd still be glad to do the work at that price. The final number
lands inside the range you said.
