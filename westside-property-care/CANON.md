## 1. The business in one paragraph

**Westside Property Care 513 (WPC513)** is a solo residential outdoor-living property-care
service on the **west side of Cincinnati, Ohio**, spun out of the family's grill-cleaning
business, Tri-State Grill Cleaning. It is **seasonal and small by design**: a deliberately
capped book of premium residential clients, served **March through October** by one person,
with capacity left over for profitable project work. It is a *deliberate side income*, not a
venture. Do not write copy or plans that imply growth, scale, franchising, crews, or
"expanding to serve more families."

Tagline: **Seasonal Outdoor Living & Property Care.**

---

## 2. The one idea everything else follows from

**The membership is not the revenue. The membership is the lead engine.**

Its job is to put a trusted operator on a property **16 times a season**, where every visit
is also an inspection that surfaces quotable work. Membership revenue covers itself;
**project revenue is the actual income.** This matters *more* under the seasonal model, not
less — an eight-month membership year makes project work the larger share.

Practical consequence: never write anything that treats the membership as the finish line.
Every touchpoint — the service report especially, and the checklist's
`NOTES / ITEMS TO QUOTE / FOLLOW-UP` block — is where the next project gets found.

## 3. Non-negotiable numbers

### Price — one number

**$279 per month.** That is the entire membership price list. There is no tier, no module, no
add-on price, and no configuration table. A property with a pool and an outdoor kitchen pays
the same as a property with neither.

- Billed monthly during the **March–October service season**.
- Card on file, charged on the schedule set at enrollment.
- **$249 is not a price in this business.** It appeared on an early flyer draft and is
  withdrawn. It also collides with the published `$249+` grill deep-clean project price.

> **Superseded.** An earlier version of this file specified four attribute-based
> configurations — $229 / $269 / $289 / $329, derived from a base plus a pool module and an
> outdoor-kitchen module. **All four are dead.** None may appear anywhere outside `_source/`.

**What the flat price means downstream:** property attributes no longer set the price, so
they become a **client-selection criterion instead**. A pool-and-outdoor-kitchen property
consumes far more of the two-hour visit than a plain patio at the same fee. Pool and outdoor
kitchen now decide *whether he takes the client*, not what he charges. See
`00-model/ideal-client.md`.

### Visit structure — the two-hour cap

**Two scheduled visits per month, up to two hours of on-site service per visit. Unused
service time does not roll over.**

The cap is not a stopwatch on quality — it is **the mechanism that makes a flat price
survivable** across properties of very different size. It travels with two clauses that must
appear wherever the cap does:

- Highest-priority maintenance items come first, using his judgment.
- **Completion of every possible task during every visit is not guaranteed.**

**How to talk about it:** the cap is a **scope boundary**, not the thing being purchased.
Lead with what stays maintained; state the cap where a boundary belongs. Never let the copy
imply the customer is buying hours — $279 ÷ 4 hours reads as an hourly rate and invites
comparison with a handyman, which is the wrong frame for the service.

### Season

**March 1 – October 31.** Eight months, two visits a month, **16 visits a season.**

- **In Season:** March 1 – October 31.
- **Off Season:** November 1 – end of February. No scheduled recurring visits.
- The 2026 season is already underway. Clients signing now get a **pro-rated remainder of
  2026 plus a locked founding rate for a full 2027.**

> **Superseded.** The earlier model was year-round with a Green Season and a Dormant Season
> at one flat price, and argued *"the same price in January as in July."* **That argument is
> now false and must not survive anywhere** — including the page footers, where it appeared
> on nine of ten pages.

There is a **separate, optional winter offering** for November–February (see
`03-marketing/winter-service.md`). It is not part of this agreement and must never be
described as included.

### Capacity

- **Launch at 4 clients. Hard cap at 6.**
- **Routine visits happen only on Monday and Friday**, on the schedule set with each client.
- **Tuesday–Thursday is project, estimate, and emergency time.** Protect it — that is where
  the money is.
- **No regularly scheduled weekend work.**
- Route hours follow from the client count and the two-hour cap; `00-model/operating-model.md`
  derives the number. The old `1.5 hours per property` planning constant is dead — the cap
  plus the heavier pool scope replaced it.
- When all slots are full, the site says **MEMBERSHIP FULL** and offers a **waitlist**. The
  scarcity is real. Never fake it, never soften it.

### Metrics — the ban, stated precisely

Track: **revenue per route day, project attach rate, membership retention, referrals.**

The earlier blanket ban on "minutes per visit" is **narrowed, not lifted.** The distinction
is real and both halves matter:

- **Legitimate — a scope boundary.** "Up to two hours of on-site service per visit" is a
  contractual limit on what is purchased. His field checklist budgets those two hours across
  eight blocks. Both are correct and both stay.
- **Still banned — a performance target.** No "average visit length," no minutes-per-visit
  tracked as a KPI, no copy implying he aims to finish quickly. That is what conflicted with
  the standard *never rush a property*, and that standard is unchanged.

One is a ceiling on the customer's entitlement. The other is a stopwatch on his work. Never
confuse them.

### The operating motto — his words, and they beat ours

> **Maintain everything. Deep-clean nothing unless specifically scheduled.**

It does more scope protection than a page of exclusions. It belongs on the checklist, in the
agreement, and on the site.

## 4. Why this is a membership and not just a seasonal service

The old answer was "the same price in January as in July." **That answer is gone**, and
nothing inherited its job. This replaces it, built only from things that are already true:

> **Sixteen visits. Six properties. The same person every time, who writes down what he saw.**

- **Six properties, ever.** The cap is real and it is the product.
- **The same person every visit.** No rotating crew, no route number, no dispatcher.
- **A written record.** Sixteen documented inspections a season, with photos. He knows what
  he saw last time, and so do you.
- **Scope stated up front.** *Maintain everything, deep-clean nothing unless scheduled* — he
  tells you what he does not do before you sign, which almost nobody does.

Use this wherever the winter-price argument used to sit.

## 5. Who the customer actually is

West-side Cincinnati home values are modest: Delhi $180–350k, Bridgetown ~$240k, Green
Township $250–450k, Westwood/Price Hill/Cheviot ~$175k. A $279/month membership addresses a
**narrow** slice of that.

- **Target:** Green Township subdivisions, Oak Hills, and the upper end of Delhi and
  Covedale — homes with a real pool or outdoor-kitchen investment and an owner who already
  pays other people to maintain things.
- **Not the target:** Westwood, Price Hill, Cheviot. Do not write copy or targeting aimed at
  them.

This narrowness is a feature. He needs **six** clients.

**The pool comparison is now a live risk, not a deflection.** Dedicated pool service in
Cincinnati runs **$121–142/month for four visits including chemicals**. The old scope
excluded nearly all pool work, so that number was easy to wave off. The new scope includes
vacuuming, brushing, backwashing, and salt-cell checks — which puts $279 in direct comparison
with a specialist at half the price. The honest answer leads with his own disclaimer: **no
guarantee of water chemistry, clarity, or equipment performance.** He maintains the pool area
as part of the whole outdoor living space; he is not a pool company and does not want to be.

## 8. Voice guardrails (full profile in `01-brand/voice.md`)

- Write like a competent tradesperson who takes pride in the work — **not** like a marketing
  department.
- Plain, concrete, specific. "Pool skimmed, baskets emptied, patio blown clean" beats
  "comprehensive outdoor living solutions."
- **Never** use: "solutions," "premier," "one-stop shop," "peace of mind" as a headline,
  "we're passionate about," "elevate your outdoor living experience."
- No exclamation marks in body copy.
- Specificity is the entire brand. He personally knows which gate sticks and where the
  window wells fill up.
- Never claim crews, years in business, client counts, or certifications that don't exist.
  No invented testimonials, no fake review counts, no made-up awards. **"Licensed" stays
  banned** — Ohio does not license this trade, so the claim is wrong rather than early.
- **"Military roots" is real and usable** (his own Trust/About note). State it plainly; never
  quantify it into ranks, years, or units that have not been confirmed.
- Name: **Westside Property Care 513** in full on first reference. **WPC513** thereafter and
  as the mark. **"WPC" alone never appears in customer-facing copy.**
