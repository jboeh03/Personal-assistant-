- **INFERENCE (scope interaction):** fascia work at the roofline is squarely in the roofing contractor's trade — if rot is found, fold it into the RS-1/RS-2/RS-3 roofer's invoice rather than paying a gutter crew's carpentry upcharge, and it also keeps the Defect Notice's "qualified professional" paper trail clean.
- **RECOMMENDATION:** in the 08-10 response, do not promise "repair" language narrower than what's found — reserve wording like "repair or replace gutter fasteners/hangers and adjacent fascia as required" so a rot discovery on ~08-15 doesn't require renegotiating the notice.

| Complication (present at this house) | Adder | Basis | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Second suction point (poor sub-slab communication; wet sub-slab soil kills pressure-field extension) | +$300–$500 typical; up to $500–$1,500 on complex foundations | ([EnviroPro360](https://enviropro360.com/blog/radon/radon-mitigation-systems-how-they-work-and-what-they-cost-2/), [HomeHazardsReport](https://www.homehazardsreport.com/blog/radon-mitigation-cost), [InspectandTest](https://inspectandtest.net/guides/radon-mitigation-cost/), [HVAC Review Hub](https://hvacreviewhub.org/radon-mitigation-system-cost-typical-prices-range/)); full multi-point systems $2,500–$5,000 total ([InspectandTest](https://inspectandtest.net/guides/radon-mitigation-cost/)) | EVIDENCE |
| Sump pit sealing (perimeter water signs make an existing/added sump likely; open pit is a major radon entry) | +$150–$250 when done with the install (dome cover, grommets, caulk); $900–$2,500 standalone | ([RadonVerdict](https://radonverdict.com/guides/sump-pump-radon-mitigation), [EraseRadon](https://eraseradon.com/blog/sump-pump-radon-entry), [PDS Radon Supply](https://radonpds.com/sump-pit-radon-mitigation-package/)) | EVIDENCE |
| Crack/slab-opening sealing (SS-1 cracks w/ movement) | ~$100–$500 as an install adder | Concrete caulking $5–$10/LF ([A-1 Concrete](https://www.a1concrete.com/concrete-repair-learning-center/concrete-caulking-cost)); passive sealing package $300–$1,500 ([Wisconsin DHS](https://www.dhs.wisconsin.gov/radon/reduce-radon.htm)). Adder band is INFERENCE: $5–$10/LF × the accessible crack footage a mitigator would caulk (roughly 20–50 LF), landing inside the $300–$1,500 package band's lower half | INFERENCE (rates are EVIDENCE) |
| Drain-tile suction variant (if perimeter drain exists / high water table) | $900–$1,800 installed — an alternative design, not an adder | ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/radon-mitigation-cost), [Radon Reduction Inc](https://radonreductioninc.com/tag/drain-tile-depressurization/)) | EVIDENCE |
| Membrane/vapor-barrier work | Not applicable here — that's the crawl-space driver ($1,800–$3,500 crawl-space band per [RadonResources](https://radonresources.com/ohio/cincinnati/)); this is a full basement | EVIDENCE (of non-applicability) |

**Two important design notes (EVIDENCE):**
- Sealing alone is not a mitigation method — it hasn't been shown to reduce radon much by itself; it is a supplement that makes ASD suction more efficient ([Wisconsin DHS](https://www.dhs.wisconsin.gov/radon/reduce-radon.htm), [SOS Radon](https://sosradon.org/reducing-radon-in-your-home)). So SS-1 cracks don't change the *method*, they add sealing labor and threaten pressure-field extension.
- Water under the slab / high water table is an explicitly listed high-end cost driver ([Radon Mitigation Cincinnati](https://radonmitigationcincinnati.com/), [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-it-cost-remove-radon-gas.htm)) and is what pushes a bid from single-point toward second-point or drain-tile designs.

**INFERENCE (this house):** 13.6 pCi/L avg (range 6–45) stays under the ~20 pCi/L severe-design threshold from v1, so no high-CFM/continuous-monitor escalation. But cracked, patched walls + perimeter water signs mean a competent bidder will likely price: standard ASD + sump/pit sealing + crack caulking, holding a second suction point as a contingency if the post-install diagnostic shows weak pressure-field extension. Stack: $1,000–$1,500 base + $150–$250 sump + $100–$500 sealing + $0–$500 second point.

### 2d. Timeline reconfirmation

Unchanged from v1 — the complications add **hours to the one-day install** (extra core hole, caulking, sump dome), not days: installs run 3–8 hours ([Radon1](https://radon1.com/how-long-radon-mitigation-takes/), [Radon Guys](https://radonguys.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-install-a-radon-system)); Cincinnati booking windows are days-to-a-week ([Cincinnati Radon Pros](https://cincinnatiradonpros.com/), [Environmental Doctor](https://envirodoc.com/radon-mitigation/)); ≥24 hr fan operation then 48-hr retest ([AARST MAH](https://standards.aarst.org/MAH-2019/6/), [SOS Radon](https://sosradon.org/real-estate-testing)).

> **CONCLUSION (reconfirmed): fits before the ~08-25 re-inspection provided the installer is booked by ~08-12** — i.e., within ~2 business days of the 08-10 agreement. Worked chain: book 08-11/08-12 → install 08-14–08-19 → fan 24 hr → 48-hr retest → result in hand 08-18–08-22, leaving 3–7 days of margin. One new caveat: if the bidder proposes a *diagnose-then-add-second-point* sequence, insist the contingency point be drilled in the same visit or within 48 hr, or the retest window compresses. ODH-licensure requirement and the <4.0 pCi/L retest deliverable stand as written in v1.
