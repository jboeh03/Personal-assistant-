### DIY / professional tension

- **INFERENCE:** Gutter cleaning and hanger tightening is the most DIY-able item in the entire Defect Notice — no trade license exists for gutter work in Ohio, and materials cost is trivial (hangers $2–$3 each per [Today's Homeowner](https://todayshomeowner.com/gutters/cost/gutter-repair-cost/)).
- **EVIDENCE (contract):** The Defect Notice requires work "completed in a professional, workmanlike manner by a professional qualified to make the agreed upon corrections" and requires work orders, warranties, receipts, and proof of payment.
- **INFERENCE (flag):** Seller-DIY gutter work likely fails the paper-trail requirement (no third-party receipt/warranty) even though it is technically trivial. The sellers' agent-as-licensed-contractor route, or bundling RS-4/RS-5 into the roofer's or a handyman service's invoice, satisfies the "professional" language at modest cost. Do not plan on homeowner DIY for the notice response unless the buyers agree in writing.
- **RECOMMENDATION:** Have the gutter scope added to whichever roofing contractor handles RS-1/RS-2/RS-3 (they will already be on the roofline) — marginal cost is likely at the low end of the band and it produces one clean invoice for the re-inspection package.

**Does 13.6 pCi/L change design/cost?**
- **EVIDENCE:** Cost guides report severe radon **over ~20 pCi/L** adds 30–50% (high-CFM fan, dual suction points, continuous monitoring) ([CostToBuildHouse](https://www.costtobuildhouse.com/radon-mitigation-cost), [RadonSeal](https://www.radonseal.com/radon/articles/mitigation-costs.htm)).
- **INFERENCE:** At 13.6 pCi/L — elevated but under the ~20 pCi/L design-escalation threshold — a standard single-suction ASD with a mid-range fan should suffice for a typical basement; expect a quote in the **$1,000–$1,800** range rather than the top of the band. Caveat: the inspection's waived items note foundation cracks and water-entry signs (SS-1/SS-2), and readings ranged 6–45 pCi/L; sub-slab communication issues could push toward a second suction point.

### Ohio radon mitigation licensure

- **EVIDENCE:** Ohio law requires a license to perform radon mitigation. ORC 3723.02 is titled "Radon tester, mitigation specialist or mitigation contractor license required — exceptions" ([ORC 3723.02](http://codes.ohio.gov/orc/3723.02)). The Ohio Department of Health licenses radon mitigation contractors, mitigation specialists (who must provide direct on-site supervision of installs), testers, and labs ([ODH Radon Licensing](https://odh.ohio.gov/know-our-programs/radon-education-and-licensing-program/radon-licensing), [OAC 3701-69-02](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-3701-69-02)).
- **INFERENCE:** Unlike the gutters, radon mitigation has **zero DIY or agent-as-contractor path** for the notice response — the installer must hold an ODH radon mitigation license, and the deliverable paperwork (work order, warranty, receipt) will come from that licensed firm. This actually simplifies compliance: any licensed installer automatically satisfies the "qualified professional" clause.

### Does "mitigate" imply a retest below 4.0?

- **EVIDENCE:** EPA-aligned real-estate guidance states the goal of negotiated mitigation is to bring the level **below 4.0 pCi/L**, and that the only way to confirm is a post-mitigation retest, ideally by an independent third party (contractor self-retesting is a conflict of interest) ([SOS Radon / National Radon Program Services](https://sosradon.org/real-estate-testing), [Radon Abatement Services FAQ](https://radonrepair.com/faq/selling-your-home/)). Mitigation contractors commonly guarantee <4.0 pCi/L in their bids ([Guardian Radon](https://www.guardianservices.biz/blog/fail-radon-test-after-mitigation/)); Cincinnati installers advertise post-install retests confirming <2.0 pCi/L ([Cincinnati Radon Pros](https://cincinnatiradonpros.com/)).
- **INFERENCE:** The notice language "Seller to mitigate radon if the level is at or over 4.0" defines the *trigger* at 4.0 but doesn't literally state a performance target. Standard Ohio/EPA real-estate practice, and the buyer's obvious intent, is **system installed + post-mitigation test result under 4.0** presented at re-inspection. Plan to deliver both; a contractor <4.0 guarantee closes any gap in the contract language.
- **RECOMMENDATION:** In the 08-10 response, agree to "installation of an active sub-slab depressurization system by an ODH-licensed radon mitigation contractor, with post-mitigation radon test result below 4.0 pCi/L provided prior to re-inspection." This is the standard ask, costs nothing extra to concede, and pre-empts a re-inspection dispute.
