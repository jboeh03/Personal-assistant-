# Insurance Angle — RS-1 "Creased As Though Wind-Bent" Shingles

**as_of: 2026-08-07** · deal-cost-analyst · Context: $585K sale, Defect Notice response due 08/10, repairs due ~08/24, re-inspection ~08/25, closing 08/28.

## 1. Is wind-creasing covered in Ohio?

- EVIDENCE: Wind is a covered peril on standard homeowners policies, and creased shingles are the textbook presentation of wind damage — wind bends the shingle back, cracking the asphalt mat, which is **functional** (not cosmetic) damage that insurers pay to fix ([InsuranceClaimHQ](https://insuranceclaimhq.com/wind-creased-shingles-damage/), [Bill Ragan Roofing](https://www.billraganroofing.com/blog/insurance-cover-roof-damage-wind), [IBEX Roof](https://www.ibexroof.com/does-insurance-cover-wind-damage-to-shingles/)).
- EVIDENCE: Common denial paths: pre-existing poor condition reframed as "maintenance," and roofs over ~20 years old receiving partial or no coverage ([IBEX Roof](https://www.ibexroof.com/does-insurance-cover-wind-damage-to-shingles/), [Brown's Roofing](https://brownsroofingla.com/learning-center/how-much-wind-damage-is-required-for-insurance-to-replace-a-roof/)).
- INFERENCE: The inspector's phrase "creased as though they have been wind-bent" is favorable claim language, but RS-2 sits next to it: buckled *sheathing* forcing shingles to lift is a maintenance/structural condition, not wind. An adjuster who sees both findings may attribute the whole picture to deferred maintenance. Coverage is plausible, not assured.

## 2. Deductible math on a ~$585K house

- EVIDENCE: Wind/hail deductibles are frequently **separate** from the all-perils deductible, set at **1%–5% of dwelling (Coverage A)** or a flat amount; Ohio is a high wind/hail state where separate wind deductibles are common ([Westfield Insurance](https://www.westfieldinsurance.com/about-us/articles/what-is-a-wind-and-hail-deductible-in-homeowners-insurance), [The Zebra](https://www.thezebra.com/resources/home/separate-deductible-for-wind-and-hail/), [United Policyholders](https://uphelp.org/homeowners-how-to-understand-a-wind-hail-deductible/?print=print)).
- INFERENCE (derivation): Coverage A unknown (null — read the declarations page). If Coverage A is $400K–$550K: 1% = **$4,000–$5,500**; 2% = **$8,000–$11,000**. RS-1 costs $350–$1,500 to fix; the *entire* five-item roof package is ~$2,500–$7,500. A 1% deductible likely exceeds the whole package; 2% certainly does. Only a **flat** deductible ($1,000–$2,500 typical) leaves any recovery, and only if the adjuster scopes beyond spot repair.
- INFERENCE: The one interesting scenario: Ohio's matching rule (below) forces a slope/full-roof replacement scope ($10K–$16K+ in Cincinnati — [Brandstetter Roofing](https://www.brandstetterroofing.com/how-much-does-roof-replacement-cost-in-cincinnati-oh/)); then even a percentage deductible could leave five figures. That is a lottery ticket, not a plan — and it collides with the timeline and the sale.

## 3. Matching and roof age (ACV vs RCV)

- EVIDENCE: Ohio has a real matching rule — OAC 3901-1-54(I)(1)(b) requires replacing enough of the item for a **"reasonably comparable appearance"** when replacements don't match; applies to roofing and siding ([Ohio Insurance Agents Assn.](https://ohioinsuranceagents.com/blog/2023/matching-in-ohio-whats-the-law/), [Property Insurance Coverage Law Blog](https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/ohio-matching-law/), [OAC rule text](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-3901-1-54)). It does **not** automatically mean full replacement ([Isaac Wiles](https://www.isaacwiles.com/blog/a-reasonable-match-is-often-but-not-always-acceptable-under-ohio-law/)).
- EVIDENCE: On an ACV policy an older roof's payout is depreciated hard (illustration: ~$5,000 on a $12,000 replacement at 15 years); RCV pays current cost minus deductible; discontinued-shingle proof takes an ITEL test $200–$400 ([Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/insurance/homeowners-insurance/roof-insurance-acv-versus-replacement-cost), [Mutual Benefit Group](https://www.mutualbenefitgroup.com/insurance-101/personal-insurance-101/roof-insurance-claims), [MWL survey](https://www.mwl-law.com/matching-regulations-affecting-homeowners-insurance-claims/)).
- null — roof age and policy type (ACV/RCV, deductible structure) for this property: not in the deal room; must be read off the sellers' declarations page.

## 4. Timeline vs the 3-week window

- EVIDENCE: Ohio claim-handling clock (OAC 3901-1-54): acknowledge within **15 days**; accept/deny within **21 days of executed proof of loss** (extendable with written 45-day updates); pay within **10 days of acceptance** ([OAC 3901-1-54](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-3901-1-54), [United Policyholders Ohio](https://uphelp.org/claim-guidance-publications/insurance-consumer-rights-in-ohio-2022/)).
- INFERENCE: Best realistic case — file 08/08, adjuster inspects within a week, acceptance ~week 3–4, check after. That lands **at or after closing**; any friction (engineer review, maintenance dispute over RS-2) pushes it months out. Meanwhile the Defect Notice requires the repair completed by ~08/24 — and repairing before the adjuster inspects destroys the claim evidence. The two clocks are fundamentally incompatible.

## 5. Claim-during-sale complications

- EVIDENCE: An open claim must be disclosed to buyers; it can affect the buyers' ability to bind their own insurance; claims can be assigned post-loss but need attorney review ([Cohen Law Group](https://itsaboutjustice.law/selling-your-home-with-an-open-insurance-claim/), [Sill Public Adjusters](https://www.sill.com/latest-news/posts/how-does-selling-a-property-impact-an-insurance-claim/)).
- INFERENCE: For this deal that's all downside: the buyers' lender needs clear-to-close ~08/25, the buyers need to bind coverage before then, and an open wind claim is a new complication introduced at the worst moment for a repair the sellers can fund from a $585K closing.

## Conclusion

**(iii) Not worth pursuing — with one cheap hedge.**

1. **Economics:** RS-1 costs $350–$1,500; the likely wind deductible ($1,000+ flat, or $4,000–$11,000 percentage) probably equals or exceeds the entire roof package. Expected net recovery ≈ $0 unless matching forces full-replacement scope — low probability and can't resolve inside this deal's timeline. — INFERENCE from cited figures.
2. **Timeline:** Ohio's 15/21/10-day claim clock can't reliably finish before 08/24, and repairing first guts the claim. Not a pre-closing remedy. — EVIDENCE + INFERENCE.
3. **Deal risk:** An open claim adds disclosure and buyer-insurance friction against committed buyers in a deal the sellers need to close. — EVIDENCE + INFERENCE.
4. It isn't even a good post-closing offset: the evidence disappears with the repair.

**RECOMMENDATION (the hedge, ~30 minutes):** Before repairs begin — (a) have the roofer photograph and note the creased shingles in writing during the estimate ("wind-creased, functional damage") so the option is preserved if major hidden wind damage surfaces; (b) one phone call to the sellers' insurance *agent* (a question, not a filed claim) to confirm wind/hail deductible structure and ACV/RCV status. If, against expectation, it's flat-$500 + RCV and the roofer scopes a slope replacement, revisit. Otherwise: pay for the repair, close on 08/28, don't look back.
