## 1. How often do pending sales actually die? (base rates)

Two national datasets measure this differently — both matter:

| Metric | Latest figure | Data date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contracts **terminated** (agent-reported, past 3 months) | **6%** (5% prior month, 6% a year ago) | June 2026 | EVIDENCE — [NAR REALTORS® Confidence Index](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/realtors-confidence-index) |
| Contracts with **delayed settlement** (past 3 months) | **13%** (14% prior month, 12% a year ago) | June 2026 | EVIDENCE — [NAR RCI](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/realtors-confidence-index) |
| Contracts **delayed by appraisal issues** | **7%** (6% prior month and prior year) | June 2026 | EVIDENCE — [NAR RCI](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/realtors-confidence-index) |
| Pending sales **canceled** (share of homes going under contract that month) | **13.4%** (~53,000 deals) — up from 12.5% a year earlier | March 2026 (latest month verified) | EVIDENCE — [Redfin, "More Than 50,000 Home-Purchase Contracts Fell Through in March"](https://www.redfin.com/news/contract-cancellations-march-2026/) |
| Recent Redfin monthly trend | Dec 2025: **16.3%** (record for any December since 2017); Jan 2026: **13.7%**; Feb 2026: **13.7%** (record for those months) | Dec 2025 – Feb 2026 | EVIDENCE — [Redfin Dec](https://www.redfin.com/news/press-releases/redfin-reports-homebuyers-are-canceling-deals-at-the-highest-rate-on-record/) · [Jan](https://www.redfin.com/news/pending-sales-fall-through-january-2026/) · [Feb](https://www.redfin.com/news/contract-cancellations-february-2026/) |

**Why the numbers differ:** Redfin counts cancellations as a share of homes that went under
contract in a given month (~13–16% recently); NAR asks agents what share of their contracts
terminated over the trailing three months (~5–7%). Different denominators and survey methods —
the honest summary for a layman is: **roughly 1 in 7 signed deals nationally has been falling
apart before closing in the current market, and it has never been higher for this era; but the
large majority — about 85%+ — still close.** (EVIDENCE, sources above.)

Redfin attributes the elevated 2025–26 cancellation rates to "stubbornly high housing costs,
layoffs, and mounting economic and political uncertainty" — i.e., market-wide buyer skittishness,
not usually anything the seller did. (EVIDENCE — [Redfin, Feb 2026 report](https://www.redfin.com/news/contract-cancellations-february-2026/).)

**Unverified:** Redfin's April–June 2026 cancellation figures could not be confirmed from this
environment (data-center page blocked). The March 2026 figure is the latest verified month.

## 2. What kills deals, ranked (with evidence)

### The best single dataset: Redfin's September 2025 agent survey
Redfin surveyed **443 of its agents** who had handled cancellations in the prior three months
(published October 2025). Agents could cite multiple causes. (EVIDENCE — [Redfin, "Homebuyers Are Canceling Deals at a Record Rate. Here's Why."](https://www.redfin.com/news/home-purchase-cancellations-august-2025/), also covered by [Fortune, Oct 13 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/10/13/housing-market-homebuyers-contract-cancelations-inspection-repairs-home-prices/).)

| Rank | Cause | % of agents citing it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Home inspection or repair issues** | **70.4%** |
| 2 | Buyer financing fell through | 27.8% |
| 3 | Buyer unable to sell their current home | 21.0% |
| 4 | Change in buyer's financial situation | 14.9% |
| 5 | Buyer found a different property they liked better | 12.9% |
| 6 | Concerns about the economic climate | 12.2% |

### Corroborating / supplementary numbers

- **Inspection dominance is consistent across sources.** A secondary breakdown attributed to NAR
  data puts inspection issues at ~25% of terminations, financing ~21%, appraisal ~15%
  (EVIDENCE, secondhand — [HomeLight](https://www.homelight.com/blog/how-often-do-pending-offers-fall-through/) and
  [Mark Hiller Sells summary](https://www.markhillersells.com/blog/top-5-reasons-home-contracts-fall-through-and-how-sellers-can-stay-ahead/); I could not
  verify the exact NAR primary table from this environment — treat the 25/21/15 split as
  indicative, not precise). One inspection-industry source cites a much lower 10–12% share from
  the 2024 NAR RCI ([Hometown Inspections](https://www.hometowninspect.com/2025/12/05/its-not-the-home-inspector-who-kills-the-deal-its-the-condition-of-the-home/)) — the spread reflects
  methodology differences, but every source ranks inspection/repair disputes at or near #1.
- **When inspections kill deals, it's major items** — structural problems, water intrusion,
  **roof failure**, unsafe electrical panels, end-of-life HVAC — not minor punch-list items.
  (EVIDENCE, industry commentary — [Hometown Inspections, Dec 2025](https://www.hometowninspect.com/2025/12/05/its-not-the-home-inspector-who-kills-the-deal-its-the-condition-of-the-home/).) Note: the Stonington buyers already
  *waived* the scary categories (foundation, panel, HVAC) and asked only for roof + radon.
- **Appraisal below contract price is rarer than people fear:** Fannie Mae's analysis of ~6.5M
  purchase appraisals (2013–2017) found the appraisal comes in **below contract in roughly 8%**
  of transactions; ~64.5% appraise above and ~32% at contract price. (EVIDENCE, older data —
  [Fannie Mae working paper](https://www.fanniemae.com/media/48121/display).) NAR's June 2026 RCI
  puts appraisal-caused *delays* at 7% of contracts. (EVIDENCE, June 2026 — [NAR RCI](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/realtors-confidence-index).)
- **Title issues are common but almost always cured, not fatal:** ALTA's 2024–2026 studies found
  title problems surface in roughly **25–36% of transactions** and nearly 60% of deals require
  clearing 3–5 title issues — but this is routine curative work done *before* closing by the
  title company; it delays far more often than it kills. (EVIDENCE — [ALTA Critical Issues Study, March 2026](https://www.alta.org/file/Measuring-the-Complexity-of-Title-Production-Survey-Summary.pdf) · [ALTA 2024 study](https://www.alta.org/media/pdf/240506-ALTA-Title-Insurance-Curative-Work-Study-Report.pdf) · [HousingWire coverage](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/alta-study-title-production-complexity-curative-work/).)
- **Home-sale contingency risk:** cited by 21% of agents in the Redfin survey (above). Not
  applicable here — the Sipniewskis' contract has no home-sale contingency in the deal brief.

## 3. Which causes can THIS seller control? (deal is past inspection-negotiation once the Defect Notice is resolved)

The single biggest killer nationally — the inspection-negotiation blowup — is exactly the phase
the Stonington deal exits on 08/10 if the response lands cleanly. What remains between response
and the 08/28 closing:

| Remaining risk | Seller-controllable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Execution of agreed repairs** (quality, timing, documentation, passing buyer re-inspection ~08/25) | **YES — almost fully** | The one remaining risk that is squarely in the sellers' hands. See §4. |
| **Appraisal below $585K** | Partially | Can't control the appraiser's number; CAN keep the home appraisable (visible roof defects fixed or disclosed-and-credited), provide the agent's comp package, and lean on the buyers' $10K appraisal-gap addendum. Base rate of low appraisal ~8% (Fannie Mae, above); INFERENCE: over-ask price on no-showings contract modestly raises the odds here. |
| **Buyer financing** (conditional approval ~08/21, CTC ~08/25) | Mostly NO | Buyer's job, credit, and lender are out of seller hands. Sellers CAN avoid giving the lender new problems: lender-visible repair credits must fit the lender's cap rules (§15(b)(d) is explicitly "up to what buyer's lender allows"), and an appraiser who notes an unrepaired, visibly damaged roof can make the loan "subject to repair" (INFERENCE from conventional-lending practice; consistent with agent commentary that lenders flag roof condition). |
| **Title** | Mostly YES for *this* file | Curative work is routine (ALTA, above). The sellers' known liens/personal debt payable from proceeds are exactly the kind of item that delays closings when payoff letters arrive late — ordering payoff statements through Fidelity Land Title NOW is fully in seller control. |
| **Buyer cold feet / remorse** | Indirectly | Can't control feelings; CAN avoid triggering them. Redfin agents note deals die when parties re-trade or stall. These buyers' signals (over-ask, $10K EMD, gap coverage, focused ask) are the profile of buyers who close. (EVIDENCE for signals: deal brief; INFERENCE on behavior.) |

## 4. Repair execution and the re-inspection — the risk that remains, and how sellers blow it

Quality label: the quantitative sources above don't isolate "agreed repairs failed re-inspection"
as a tracked statistic — this section is **agent/industry commentary (moderate quality, directionally
consistent across independent sources), not survey data.**

- If agreed repairs aren't complete or verifiable at re-inspection/walk-through, the standard
  outcomes are: **delayed closing, escrow holdback from seller proceeds, renegotiation, or (worst
  case) breach-of-contract exposure** — buyers can refuse to close, demand damages, or terminate
  with EMD returned. (EVIDENCE, practitioner guidance — [FastExpert](https://www.fastexpert.com/blog/what-happens-if-a-seller-does-not-make-repairs-before-closing/) · [HomeLight](https://www.homelight.com/blog/buyer-seller-did-not-complete-repairs-before-closing/) · [Domondon RE](https://www.domondonre.com/home-repairs-are-not-completed-upon-reinspection-what-to-do).)
- Fixing problems discovered at walk-through is "much harder to accomplish after funds transfer" —
  which is why buyers' agents force the delay *before* signing, blowing hard deadlines.
  (EVIDENCE, practitioner guidance — [ARAG Legal](https://www.araglegal.com/individuals/learning-center/topics/home-and-property/spot-a-problem-at-final-walk-through).)
- The Stonington Defect Notice makes this contractual, not optional: work must be
  professional/workmanlike **by a qualified professional**, with **receipts/warranties due at the
  buyer re-inspection 3 days before closing** (~08/25). A DIY or handyman roof repair without
  paper fails that standard even if the work is fine. (EVIDENCE — deal brief/Defect Notice.)

### Seller behaviors correlated with deals that close (practitioner consensus)

1. **Respond fast, completely, and in writing** to the repair request — countering beats refusing,
   and slow/partial responses are what convert solvable issues into cancellations. (EVIDENCE,
   practitioner guidance — [Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/learn/negotiating-after-home-inspection/) · [Lamacchia Realty](https://www.lamacchiarealty.com/repairs-after-inspection/).)
2. **Use licensed contractors and keep every receipt/invoice/warranty**; bring the repair
   addendum, receipts, and invoices to re-inspection and walk-through. Documentation is what
   converts "did you really fix it?" into a 10-minute checkbox. (EVIDENCE, practitioner guidance —
   [HomeLight](https://www.homelight.com/blog/after-home-inspection-what-next/) · [US Realty Training walkthrough checklist](https://www.usrealtytraining.com/blogs/final-walkthrough-before-closing).)
3. **Credits reduce re-inspection conflict** — agents note sellers often prefer credits precisely
   because there is nothing to re-inspect and nothing to argue about at walk-through. (EVIDENCE,
   practitioner guidance — [Clever](https://listwithclever.com/real-estate-blog/reasonable-requests-after-home-inspection/).)
4. **Don't re-trade.** Buyer surveys show "change of heart"/found-another-house exits (12.9% in
   the Redfin agent survey) spike when deals stall or reopen; every renegotiation round is another
   exit ramp. (EVIDENCE for the stat; INFERENCE on mechanism.)
5. **Keep the property insurable and appraisable** — clean, accessible, utilities on, visible
   defects either repaired or papered with a credit, so neither the appraiser nor the buyer's
   insurer generates a new condition. (INFERENCE from lending/appraisal practice.)
6. **Get title moving early** — payoff statements for every lien ordered now, since payoffs
   appear in >90% of transactions and late payoff figures are a classic closing-delay cause.
   (EVIDENCE for prevalence — [ALTA study](https://www.alta.org/file/Measuring-the-Complexity-of-Title-Production-Survey-Summary.pdf); INFERENCE for the recommendation.)

## 5. What this means for the Stonington deal — mapping failure causes to response branches

Branch definitions (from the options analysis): **A** = radon mitigation performed + roof handled
as credit/price reduction; **B** = seller performs ALL requested repairs (roof package + radon)
pre-closing; **C** = all-cash credit/price reduction in lieu of all repairs.

| Failure cause (national weight) | Branch A (radon done + roof credit) | Branch B (seller performs all repairs) | Branch C (all-cash credit) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inspection/repair blowup — 70.4% of dead deals** | Mostly defused: the ask is answered; radon has an objective pass/fail retest (<4.0 pCi/L), roof dispute is converted to money. Small risk: buyers wanted repairs, not cash — a counter restarts the 2-day Settlement Period clock and *can* auto-terminate if not signed. | Fully defused *if accepted as-asked* (option (a) ends negotiation with no settlement-period risk) — but the risk moves downstream to execution (next row). | Same settlement-period risk as A but larger: buyers explicitly asked for repairs ("Seller to have roofing contractor…"), so an all-cash counter is the furthest from their ask and the most likely to feel like a re-trade. |
| **Repair execution fails re-inspection (~08/25)** (untracked statistically; the big practitioner-cited closing-derailer, §4) | Low exposure: only radon mitigation to execute — standardized, 1–2 day install by licensed mitigator, verifiable by retest number. | **Highest exposure.** Roof package (6 line items) + radon by ~08/24, by "qualified professionals," with receipts/warranties, in a 2-week August window — contractor scheduling is the whole risk. A failed or undocumented item at re-inspection = delay/holdback/renegotiation 3 days before closing. Mitigate: book contractors before signing, receipts filed as work completes. | **Zero exposure.** Nothing to re-inspect. This is the branch's core virtue. |
| **Buyer financing (27.8%)** | Neutral-to-good: repair credits/price cuts must fit lender rules — §15(b)(d) is capped at "what buyer's lender allows"; a price *reduction* (option (c)) is lender-safe, a large *credit* may exceed conventional interested-party-contribution caps (INFERENCE; verify with Zach West's lender before choosing credit vs. price cut). | Best for financing: nothing for the lender to cap, and a repaired roof removes any appraiser "subject to repair" condition risk. | Same lender-cap caution as A, at a larger dollar figure — most important branch to pre-clear with the buyer's lender. |
| **Appraisal below $585K (~8% base rate; 7% of contracts delayed)** | Mixed: if structured as price reduction, the lower price mechanically shrinks gap risk; if structured as credit, contract price stays $585K and visible roof damage remains for the appraiser to note (INFERENCE). Buyers' $10K gap coverage cushions all branches. | Best: home presents at its best; no visible defect for the appraiser to condition the loan on. | Weakest: full $585K price stands AND the appraiser can see unrepaired wind-creased shingles/buckled sheathing — the branch most likely to draw a repair condition or low number (INFERENCE from lending practice). |
| **Buyer cold feet / found another house (12.9%)** | Low: fast, responsive answer inside the 3-day window signals good faith. | Lowest: giving buyers exactly what they asked for is the strongest anti-cold-feet move available. | Moderate: cash-instead-of-repairs can read as "seller doesn't want to deal with the roof," inviting second thoughts — offset by pricing the credit generously and attaching contractor bids showing the number is real (INFERENCE). |
| **Title (25–36% of deals need curative work; rarely fatal)** | Identical across branches — branch-independent. The sellers' known liens are the live item: order payoff letters through Fidelity Land Title this week regardless of which branch is chosen. | Same | Same |
| **Home-sale contingency (21%)** | Not applicable — buyers have none. | Same | Same |

**Bottom line (INFERENCE, assumptions: buyers behave consistently with their signals to date;
contractor capacity in Cincinnati in August is tight but findable):** The national #1 deal-killer
— the inspection fight — is already 90% behind this deal; these buyers made a narrow, reasonable
ask. What remains is an execution problem, and every remaining controllable risk is minimized by
some branch: **B carries the most repair-execution risk but the least negotiation, financing, and
appraisal risk; C is the mirror image; A splits the difference by executing the one repair with
an objective pass/fail test (radon) and converting the messy one (roof) to money.** Under every
branch, the three behaviors most correlated with closing are the same: respond before the 08/10
deadline, paper everything with licensed-contractor receipts, and never reopen a settled point.
