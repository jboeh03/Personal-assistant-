## 2. Seasonality — what an August→October relist walks into

- EVIDENCE: National data show late-May listings sell for ~1.6% more (~$5,600 on a typical home); May–June bring the highest prices and fastest closings. [Zillow — Best time to sell](https://www.zillow.com/learn/best-time-to-sell/)
- EVIDENCE: Seller premiums decline into fall — September ~9.5% and October ~8.8%, notably below spring/early-summer peaks; October also carries the largest standing inventory of the year (fresh listings plus spring leftovers), giving buyers leverage. [TheClose — Best & worst times to sell](https://theclose.com/best-and-worst-time-to-sell-house/), [Opendoor — Best month to sell](https://www.opendoor.com/articles/best-month-to-sell-a-house)
- EVIDENCE (qualitative): Post-Labor-Day sellers compete against motivated carryover sellers who missed their summer price. [Opendoor — timing factors](https://www.opendoor.com/articles/8-key-factors-that-determine-the-best-time-to-sell-your-home)

**INFERENCE (assumptions: Midwest metro seasonality tracks national curves; Cincinnati's Jan-2026 momentum doesn't repeal seasonality):** A mid-August relist means marketing into September–October — the softening leg of the year — with rising local inventory. Expect a seasonal drag of roughly **1–3% on achievable price** and slower, choosier buyer traffic versus the late-July window in which the current contract was struck. This is a directional estimate, not a measured Cincinnati statistic.

## 3. Relist / back-on-market stigma

- EVIDENCE (academic, single study — moderate quality, not Cincinnati-specific): Research presented at the AEA finds relisted properties suffer a **price decrease of slightly under 4%**, and relistings accumulate a mean cumulative days-on-market of ~223 days vs. ~105 for single-listing sales. [AEA 2024 conference paper](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2024/program/paper/KBes53TE)
- EVIDENCE (industry commentary — lower quality, directionally consistent): Buyers and agents treat back-on-market homes as damaged goods; interested buyers "without a doubt" expect a credit or price cut, and investors probe with lowballs. [Brett Stomps — BOM pros/cons](https://brettstomps.com/real-estate-guides/pros-cons-to-going-back-on-market-bom/), [U.S. News — back-on-market deals](https://money.usnews.com/loans/mortgages/articles/homebuyers-are-getting-cold-feet-how-to-cash-in-on-back-on-market-deals), [HomeLight — relisting](https://www.homelight.com/blog/relisting-your-home/), [NAR — relisting tips](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/sales-and-marketing/from-delisting-to-relisting-7-tips-to-freshen-up-the-homes-marketing)

**INFERENCE (assumptions: stigma effects at this price point resemble the studied averages; the "fell through after inspection" signal is the worst variant of BOM because buyers correctly infer condition issues):** A relist of this specific house — back on market days after an inspection-period blowup — should be modeled at a **2–4% price penalty** ($12K–$23K on $585K) before any defect-specific negotiation, with materially longer marketing time than the instant sale achieved in July.

## 4. Disclosure drag — what a relisted buyer pool extracts

**EVIDENCE (law):** Ohio ORC §5302.30 requires the Residential Property Disclosure Form to disclose, within the seller's actual knowledge, the condition of roof and foundation, material defects, and hazardous substances **specifically including radon**. All findings in the 08/04 AA Home Inspection report and the 13.6 pCi/L radon result are now within the sellers' actual knowledge and must be disclosed on relist. [ORC 5302.30](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5302.30), [DocJacket Ohio disclosure summary](https://www.docjacket.com/resources/state-real-estate-disclosure-requirements/ohio)

The disclosed set on relist: roof package (wind-creased shingles, buckled sheathing ≥3 locations, missing kick-out flashing, gutter slope, chimney crown), **radon 13.6 pCi/L (3.4× action level)**, foundation cracks with visible movement + basement water signs (PE eval recommended), Wadsworth panel, furnace beyond design life, plus the minor items.

Benchmark repair costs (EVIDENCE): radon mitigation typically **$787–$1,281**, most ~$1,000 ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-it-cost-remove-radon-gas.htm), [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/environmental-safety/remove-radon-gas)); typical seller radon concession in negotiations runs $1,500–$3,000 ([HomeLight — selling with radon](https://www.homelight.com/blog/is-it-hard-to-sell-a-house-with-radon/)); roof flashing repairs $200–$600, chimney flashing $300–$1,800, sheathing replacement $2–4/sq ft, typical roof repairs $475–$3,400 ([HomeGuide roof repair](https://homeguide.com/costs/roof-repair-cost), [HomeGuide chimney flashing](https://homeguide.com/costs/chimney-flashing-repair-cost), [Modernize flashing](https://modernize.com/roof/repair-cost/flashing)).

**INFERENCE — the asymmetry (assumptions: contractor pricing at the midpoints above; a fall buyer pool that reads the full disclosure without the current buyers' emotional commitment):**

| Scenario | What it costs the sellers |
|---|---|
| **Current buyers' ask** (roof package + radon only; foundation/panel/HVAC consciously waived) | ~**$4K–$10K** in contractor work |
| **Relist buyer pool** (full disclosure, no waivers pre-committed) | Radon credit $1.5K–$3K; roof $5K–$10K (buyers pad known defects); **foundation "cracks with visible movement" + PE-eval language $5K–$15K+ of price fear or demanded remediation**; Wadsworth panel $2K–$4K; end-of-life furnace $3K–$8K of priced-in risk. Items overlap and no single buyer stacks all of them fully — but the disclosure packet reads badly as a whole. Plausible combined extraction: **$15K–$30K** below whatever the relist price is — which itself starts 2–4% below $585K (Section 3). |

The current Defect Notice is the *cheap* version of this negotiation: the scariest disclosure items (foundation movement, panel, HVAC) are already waived in writing by committed buyers.

## 6. Buyer-quality comparison

**In hand (EVIDENCE, deal file):** over-ask $585K reached without further showings; $10K earnest; $10K appraisal-gap coverage; owner-occupants; a Defect Notice that deliberately waived foundation, panel, and HVAC and asked only for roof + radon.

**On relist (INFERENCE from Sections 1–4):** The next buyer shops in a market where only ~20% of Cincinnati homes sell over list (down from ~32% — [Houzeo](https://www.houzeo.com/housing-market/ohio/cincinnati)), inventory is up ~32% YoY ([RAGC](https://cincyrealtoralliance.com/greater-cincinnati-housing-market-kicks-off-2026-with-strong-pricing-and-growing-opportunity/)), it's fall, the listing is flagged back-on-market after inspection, and the disclosure form now recites roof defects, radon at 3.4× the action level, and foundation cracks with movement. The probability of replicating *this* buyer profile — over ask, gap coverage, narrow repair ask — under those conditions is **low; well under even odds**. The realistic relist buyer offers at or below list, negotiates the full disclosed defect set, and may involve investors probing for distress.

## Bottom line

**INFERENCE — assumptions:** relist penalty 2–4% of $585K (Section 3) partially overlapping a $15K–$30K defect-set extraction (Section 4) versus a $4K–$10K cost to satisfy the current ask; carrying $2,500–$3,500/mo for 2–3.5 extra months (Section 5); fall seasonal drag embedded in the bands; no forced-sale distress beyond that.

> **Walking away is worth roughly $20,000–$45,000 less than closing this deal** (net of the ~$4K–$10K it costs to satisfy the current roof + radon ask), **plus roughly 2–3.5 months (~$5K–$13K) of additional carrying cost and uncertainty** — through a separation, a fall market, a back-on-market flag, and a mandatory disclosure packet that now includes every finding the current buyers agreed to waive.

**RECOMMENDATION:** Treat the current buyers as the best realistic outcome available. Negotiate the *mechanics* of the roof + radon ask (scope, contractor, escrow/credit per options (b)–(e)) — but do not let the negotiation reach auto-termination; every credible walk-away scenario prices out worse than a full-price concession on the entire ask.
