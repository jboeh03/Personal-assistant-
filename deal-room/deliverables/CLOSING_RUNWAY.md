## Zach's read: "not even getting an appraisal… already given the clear to close… done deal"

Both halves are plausible. Neither should be treated as confirmed until it's in writing.

**Appraisal waiver — plausible, and very good news if true.** At a $510,000 loan on a
$585,000 price (≈87% LTV, owner-occupied, conventional), the buyers are squarely in the
range where an automated waiver ("value acceptance") is routinely granted on a strong file.
If waived, the appraisal-gap risk disappears entirely and the §19 addendum (buyers covering
up to $10,000 of gap) becomes moot. **This removes the second-biggest killer of deals at
this stage.**

**Clear-to-close on 08/13 — fast but possible**, and worth two cautions:

1. **CTC is not irrevocable.** Lenders re-verify employment and re-pull credit shortly
   before closing. A new car loan, a furniture credit line, a job change, or a large
   unexplained deposit can un-clear a cleared file. Nothing for you to do except know that
   "clear to close" on day 13 is not the same as "funded."
2. **The credit came after.** The buyers signed Addendum #2 at 9:20 and 10:33 PM on 08/12.
   If CTC was issued before that, the lender's file may not yet reflect the $6,325 seller
   credit. It has to land on the Closing Disclosure or it does not get paid — and you'd be
   sorting it out at the table. The amount itself is no obstacle: $6,325 is 1.1% of the
   sale price, far inside any interested-party-contribution limit that could apply (even
   the most restrictive tier would allow roughly $11,700 here). A revised CD for a seller
   credit does **not** restart a 3-day waiting period, so there's no delay risk either —
   but somebody has to actually send it to the lender.

**One email closes both.** Ask Zach to get Amy to confirm in writing: (a) appraisal waived
or not required, (b) clear-to-close issued and its date, (c) the lender has Addendum #2 and
the $6,325 credit is reflected on the Closing Disclosure.

## What actually remains — in priority order

### 1. Payoff letters and lien releases — the real risk now (SELLER SIDE, START NOW)

This is the item most likely to delay *your* closing, and it is entirely on your side of the
table. Fidelity Land Title needs:

- A **fresh Rocket Mortgage payoff good through 08/28+** (the one on file is dated 08/06;
  per-diem interest accrues ~$25/day, so a stale figure short-funds the payoff).
- **Payoff figures and release commitments for all six judgment liens.** These are the slow
  ones — each creditor's counsel has to produce a figure that includes post-judgment
  interest and costs, and then commit to filing a release. Two weeks is not generous for
  six of them.

**Action: call Jackie McGuire at Fidelity today and confirm all seven payoffs are ordered.**
If any creditor is slow, you want to know on 08/15, not 08/27.

### 2. Radon mitigation — your only remaining performance obligation

Line 1 of Addendum #2 is unconditional: *"Seller agrees to have radon mitigation system
installed."* Not doing it is a breach. Velocity is quoted ~$1,400–1,500 and you said it was
lined up for next week.

- Get it **installed by ~08/21** — the buyer re-inspection is ~08/25 and you want slack.
- Keep the **work order, warranty, receipt, and proof of payment** — the Defect Notice
  requires them at re-inspection.
- **Ask about a post-mitigation retest.** The Defect Notice framed this as "mitigate if the
  level is at or over 4.0" (yours was 13.6). A system installed but never retested is the
  kind of thing that turns into an argument at the walk-through. Most mitigators include or
  cheaply add a post-install test; a result under 4.0 in hand ends the conversation.

- **No repair obligations other than radon** are listed as seller responsibilities.
- Seller charges per §23 (transfer/conveyance fee, deed prep, seller-side title fees) and

this whole process. Do not skip this because the deal feels done.
