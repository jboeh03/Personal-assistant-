## What's wrong (one paragraph)

The typed terms overflow the form's line width and clip at the right margin. On the page a
person actually reads, the operative sentence ends at *"…in an amount **not**"* — the
"to exceed $6,325" is in the PDF's field data but **invisible in print**. As signed and
flattened, it reads as an open-ended promise to pay the buyers' settlement charges.
Separately, "RS-1, RS-2, EG-2-6" doesn't name RS-3, RS-4, RS-5, RS-6 — $3,753 of the
$6,325 quote.

Per contract §28, a modification is invalid unless written and signed by all parties — so
this has to be a signed addendum, not an email agreement.

## If Zach pushes back ("it's fine, the number's in there")

Two things to say, in order:

1. **The signed document is the printed one.** DocuSign flattens the form on completion —
   the earlier 1:31pm envelope is already flattened, and the clipped text is permanent
   there. Whatever a title company, the lender's underwriter, or a judge pulls up will end
   at "in an amount not."
2. **The downside is asymmetric.** Re-typing costs one DocuSign round today. Not re-typing
   risks an argument over an uncapped credit at a table on 08/28, with liens to clear from
   proceeds and no time to fight about it.

## Do not

- Do not let the fix push past **08/14**. If a clean re-issue can't be signed by all four
  in time, take the buyers' signature on the current version and paper it with Addendum #3
  after — a binding imperfect agreement beats an auto-termination under §15(c).
