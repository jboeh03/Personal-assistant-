## What would change the ranking

1. **A roofer commits in writing by 08/09 to complete by ~08/22** → (a)/(b) feasibility jumps from 3 to 4–5; (b)-amended would tie or beat H2 (buyers demonstrably prefer completed work — psych 5). EVIDENCE basis: scheduling was the binding constraint, not the work (1–2 crew-days).
2. **Buyers' lender rejects the holdback** (or drags on written approval) → (e) and H1's escrow leg die; H2/(d) become the only 08/28-safe structures. The contract analyst's late-rejection scenario is exactly why the escrow leg must be conditioned inside the Settlement Period.
3. **Appraisal comes back "subject to repairs"** on the roof → pre-closing completion is forced regardless of the §15 deal; (b)-amended becomes mandatory and credits/escrows can't substitute. (§19 gap coverage addresses value, not condition.)
4. **Buyers' actual closing costs + prepaids are smaller than the credit** → (d)/H2's credit is double-capped; shift the residual to a §(c) price reduction in the same counter (form permits combined framing — confirm with Zach).
5. **First real Cincinnati quote lands outside the band** (roof >$7,500, or attic reveals widespread decking rot) → (a) becomes materially more dangerous, H2's credit needs resizing before the counter goes out — get at least one real quote before 08/10 if humanly possible.
6. **Insurance surprise** — flat ~$500 wind deductible + RCV policy + roofer scopes slope replacement → run the insurance-angle hedge (photos + agent phone call, no filed claim) before repairs; per [insurance-angle.md](../research/insurance-angle.md) expected recovery is ≈$0 and the claim clock can't fit this deal, so this changes cost recovery, not the 08/10 response.
7. **Buyers reject any money-in-lieu on principle** ("we asked for repairs") during Settlement-Period phone pre-negotiation → skip straight to (b)-amended on 08/10 rather than burning the 2-day fuse discovering this in writing.

## Buyer-psychology model (strategist's explicit model)

The Sipniewskis waived foundation cracks with visible movement, basement water signs, an antiquated Wadsworth panel, and an end-of-design-life furnace — the *scary-sounding* items — and asked only for the roof package and radon. That is not defect-hunting or a renegotiation play; it is a committed owner-occupant couple protecting the envelope of a house they already decided to buy (EVIDENCE: Defect Notice scope vs. inspection report). Their walk-away cost is real and self-imposed: over-ask at $585K without competing showings, $10K earnest at Fidelity, $10K appraisal-gap coverage, and their names already on the deed line (EVIDENCE: contract §3, §19, deal brief). Buyers in that posture want *certainty of remedy*, not maximal extraction — a credible, fully-funded fix (completed work, a properly labeled credit, or lender-blessed escrowed funds) most likely lands, and the radon number (13.6 pCi/L, 3.4× the action level, in a home they will occupy) is the emotionally loaded item where actual completed mitigation with a <4.0 retest buys disproportionate goodwill. The corresponding risk boundary: this same commitment converts to distrust if the sellers look like they're dodging (agent-as-contractor receipts, DIY gutter work, option (f) posturing) — so every element of the response must come with a licensed name, a paper trail, or a dollar figure attached. Update this model only on new evidence from the deal room (e.g., how Amy Broghamer reacts to the phone pre-negotiation on 08/08–08/09).
