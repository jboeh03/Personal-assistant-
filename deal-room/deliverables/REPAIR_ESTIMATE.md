## The two numbers that tame the tails

1. **The re-roof cap:** this can't run away forever. A full architectural re-roof on this house runs ~$11,000–$18,000 in Cincinnati. If both roof tail-risks fire (dead colorway + spreading rot), the rational move is negotiating a re-roof or credit at that number — never $21K+ of stacked slope repairs.
2. **The Erie offset:** flat $1,000 deductible + guaranteed replacement cost + 2014 roof + $10K siding/roofing restoration endorsement = a documented wind claim can recover much of the roof cost after closing. **Photograph the creased shingles before any repair.** The bigger the roof number gets, the more this matters.

## Cheap insurance against the surprise scenarios (do these before signing bids)

- Ask every roofer bidding: "Is our 2014 shingle color still made?" (supplier check or ITEL lab match) — and get the buyers' agent to accept **"closest available match"** in writing. That one email caps RS-1 at ~$1,800.
- Get each bidder's **per-sheet change-order rate in writing** before work starts (caps RS-2 surprises at known unit prices).
- Lock the chimney scope in writing: full cast crown replacement + flashing = RS-6 satisfied; waived masonry items (EG-1 brick veneer) stay excluded. Chimney-company invoice, not roofer/agent.
- Fold gutters into the roofer's invoice (they're already on the roofline) and ask them to flag fascia condition during the estimate — before it's a mid-job discovery.
- Radon: 2–3 ODH-licensed installer quotes; ask each about second-suction-point pricing given basement crack/water history; independent retest.
